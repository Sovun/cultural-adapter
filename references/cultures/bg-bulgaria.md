# Bulgaria

## In brief

Bulgarian professional readers expect a courteous, fairly formal first message: "Уважаеми господин Петров," or "Уважаема госпожо Петрова," with the polite "Вие" form, titles where the reader has them, and "С уважение," at the end. Trust is personal and builds over several contacts, so a short courteous opening, a clear reason for writing and a modest, evidence-backed offer work better than enthusiasm or pressure. Decisions sit high in the hierarchy and a friendly reply is not a commitment; confirm in writing who decides and by when. Write in real Bulgarian, never adapted Russian or Macedonian, and keep history (North Macedonia, the Ottoman period, the 1980s campaign against Bulgarian Turks, Russia) out of copy unless it is the subject.

## Voice and tone

- Serious, polite and factual in the first exchanges; warmth comes with the relationship, then tone loosens quickly.
- Plain modern Bulgarian reads best. Avoid bureaucratic phrasing ("оказвам съдействие" instead of "помагам", "последващ" instead of "следващ"); prefer active voice and short sentences.
- Translated copy that follows English word order, overuses "Вашият" for every "your", or calques idioms is noticed at once. Rewrite from the meaning.
- Never reuse Russian, Macedonian or Serbian text "with small fixes". Bulgarian has no noun cases, uses definite articles at the end of the word (офисът, компанията) and different vocabulary; readers see a mismatch instantly.
- Check fonts: Bulgarian Cyrillic has its own lowercase shapes (д, ж, к, л, т and others) in fonts that support it. Unsupported fonts fall back to Russian forms, which is acceptable; missing glyphs or Latin look-alikes are not.

## Addressing people

- Name order is given name, (patronymic), surname: Иван Петров Георгиев. Female surnames end in -ова, -ева, -ска, -ина; the surname changes with gender, so "Госпожа Петров" is wrong.
- First contact: "Уважаеми господин Петров," / "Уважаема госпожо Петрова,". With a title: "Уважаеми проф. Иванов,", "Уважаема д-р Димитрова,", "Уважаема доц. Николова,". Abbreviations "г-н / г-жа" are common in running text and routine emails; the full form is more courteous in a first letter.
- Unknown reader: "Уважаеми дами и господа," or a function ("Уважаеми колеги от отдел „Продажби“,"). Avoid "До когото може да се отнася", which sounds translated.
- Ongoing business email: "Здравейте, г-жо Петрова," or, once on first names, "Здравей, Мария,". "Здрасти" is for friends only.
- Pronouns: "Вие" with plural verbs ("Бихте ли потвърдили...") by default. Capital "Вие/Ви/Вашият" for one reader in a formal letter is a recognised courtesy; lowercase is normal in everyday email and UI. Do not switch to "ти" until the other side does.
- Engineers often sign as "инж." and doctors as "д-р"; keep these titles when replying, especially to public institutions, universities and hospitals.
- Sign-offs: "С уважение," (formal default), "Поздрави," (colleagues), "Благодаря предварително," (when asking), "Хубав ден," (friendly, established contacts).

## Structuring a message

1. Subject line naming the topic and any reference or contract number.
2. Salutation, one courtesy line ("Благодаря Ви за срещата във вторник.").
3. Reason for writing ("Пиша Ви във връзка с...") and the request.
4. Facts, figures and attachments in short paragraphs or a list.
5. Next step with a date, who decides, offer of a call; closing formula.

- Follow every meeting or call with a written summary of agreed points, owners and dates. Paperwork, signed and stamped documents and clear contract terms carry weight.
- A positive answer in a meeting often still needs the owner's or head office's approval; ask whose sign-off is needed instead of pushing your contact.
- Headings and buttons use sentence case. Months and weekdays are lowercase (вторник, 7 март).

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Disagree with the proposal, not the person, and give a reason: "Разбирам логиката, но при този срок рискът за качеството е висок. Предлагам...".
- Criticism goes privately to the person concerned; do not copy their manager on a complaint as a first step.
- A refusal from the Bulgarian side may come as silence, short replies, "ще помислим" or "ще видим". Ask a concrete question with a date ("Да разбирам ли, че...?") instead of reading it as consent.
- Detailed questions and slow review mean the offer is being taken seriously, not rejected. Visible impatience or "decide today" ultimatums damage trust.
- Avoid lecturing about corruption, bureaucracy or "how things work here", even when local partners joke about it themselves.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- B2B trust comes from introductions, references, local presence and realistic promises. Lead with relevant cases and numbers; keep adjectives few.
- Consumer copy may be warmer and use humour, but claims still need proof; many readers distrust ads that promise too much.
- Sales materials, product information, labels and instructions must be in Bulgarian (consumer protection law); a foreign-language version may accompany it.
- An advertising self-regulation council applies an industry ethics code; EU rules on misleading and comparative advertising apply. Gambling advertising is the subject of a 2026 government plan for sharp limits; check current law.
- Marketing email and SMS need prior consent and a working opt-out under EU data protection rules.
- CV: one to two pages, reverse chronological, no superlatives. Europass is expected for public institutions, EU projects and academia; private and international employers accept a clean custom layout. A photo is optional. Write in Bulgarian for local employers, English for multinationals and IT.
- Job ads: discrimination by sex, age, ethnicity, religion, family status or appearance is prohibited and the equality commission can fine. Avoid "млада и усмихната", age ranges or gendered titles; give both forms or a neutral title ("Служител/ка обслужване на клиенти"). Readers look for salary, contract type and social benefits; EU pay-transparency rules apply from 2026, check the transposition.

## Humor and emphasis

- No jokes in a first written contact; self-deprecating humour appears once a relationship exists. Never aim jokes at the partner, their company or Bulgaria.
- No exclamation marks in professional and UI text; emphasise with stronger words or bold. No ALL CAPS.
- Humour about ethnic groups (Roma, Turks, Pomaks) or neighbouring nations is off-limits in any public text.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **North Macedonia:** language and history are an open political dispute. Avoid statements about the Macedonian language or shared historical figures; use the country's official name.
- **Ottoman period:** described in public memory as a long foreign domination; wording is debated, so use neutral terms ("Ottoman rule") and avoid romantic "Ottoman heritage" marketing.
- **Bulgarian Turks and the "Revival Process":** the 1984-89 forced renaming and expulsion of Bulgarian Turks was condemned by parliament in 2012 and remains painful. Never treat Muslim or Turkish-speaking Bulgarians as foreigners.
- **Russia and the communist past:** opinions are divided along generational and political lines. 3 March (liberation from Ottoman rule, 1878) is the national day; do not tie it to present-day politics in copy.
- **Identity:** Bulgaria is a Slavic-speaking EU and NATO member with its own language. The Cyrillic script took shape in the medieval Bulgarian state; calling it "the Russian alphabet" offends.
- **Religion:** Orthodox Christianity is the main faith, with a sizeable Muslim minority. Do not use icons, churches or clergy as jokes or props.
- Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- Traditionally a side-to-side head shake means "yes" and a nod "no"; many people adapt with foreigners. Confirm agreements in words and in writing.
- Odd numbers of flowers for happy occasions; even numbers and chrysanthemums are for funerals.
- 1 March brings red-and-white "мартеници" (Baba Marta); name days are celebrated like birthdays and a short greeting to a contact is appreciated.
- Key dates: 1 January, 3 March, Orthodox Easter (often weeks after Western Easter), 1 May, 6 May (St George, Army Day), 24 May (alphabet and culture), 6 September (Unification), 22 September (Independence), 24-26 December. A holiday next to a weekend often becomes a long weekend with a working Saturday.

## Variation within the culture

- **Sofia vs regions:** Sofia and the large cities (Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas) are faster and more international; outside them, interpreters and Bulgarian-only materials are more often needed, and relationships matter even more.
- **Sectors:** Sofia's large IT and outsourcing sector runs in English with first names, "ти" and flat teams. Banks, law, state bodies and traditional family firms keep "Уважаеми", titles and "Вие", and decide more slowly.
- **Generations:** professionals under about 40 often have Western work experience and expect quick, informal email; older readers may see early "ти" as disrespect. Older readers often learned Russian at school; younger ones English.
- **Minorities:** Bulgarian Turks (around 8%, mainly in the north-east and south-east), Pomaks and Roma. Business language is Bulgarian; regional consumer content can respectfully acknowledge Muslim holidays (Ramazan Bayram, Kurban Bayram).
- **Diaspora:** large communities in Western Europe and the US; diaspora-facing copy can mix Bulgarian and the host language.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Text is in genuine Bulgarian (articles, vocabulary), not Russian, Macedonian or Serbian with edits; Cyrillic renders correctly.
- [ ] First contact: "Уважаеми господин / Уважаема госпожо" + surname or title, gendered surname correct, comma after.
- [ ] "Вие" forms throughout until the reader uses "ти"; titles from the reader's signature (д-р, проф., инж.) kept.
- [ ] A courtesy line and reason for writing come before the request; the request has a date and asks who decides.
- [ ] Refusals and criticism give a reason and an alternative; no manager in CC on a first complaint; no ultimatums.
- [ ] Claims are backed by numbers, cases or references; no unsupported "лидер", "най-добрият", hype or exclamation marks.
- [ ] Meeting outcomes are confirmed in writing with owners and dates; head gestures are never the only proof of a "yes".
- [ ] Consumer materials, labels and instructions are in Bulgarian; marketing email goes only to opted-in recipients.
- [ ] Job ads contain no sex, age, appearance or family criteria; titles are neutral or give both forms; salary information is considered.
- [ ] Nothing on North Macedonia's language, the Ottoman period, the "Revival Process", Russia or communism unless it is the subject; Cyrillic is not called Russian.
- [ ] Dates 07.03.2026 г., times 14:30, numbers 1 234,50, prices in euro (€/EUR), quotes „ “, lowercase months, sentence-case headings.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (fast, US-style pitch):

> Hi Georgi! Loved our chat!! We're the #1 cloud partner in the region and honestly this is a no-brainer. Can you get me a yes by Friday?

**After** (to an IT director after a first meeting, in Bulgarian with gloss):

> Уважаеми господин Стоянов,
>
> Благодаря Ви за срещата в четвъртък и за отделеното време. Както обсъдихме, изпращам Ви приложено предложението с два варианта на внедряване и резултатите от три сходни проекта, които реализирахме в България през последните две години. Ако в решението участват и други колеги, моля, кажете ми на кого още да изпратя документите. Удобно ли Ви е да проведем кратък разговор в сряда, 18 март, от 10:00 ч.?
>
> С уважение,

*(Gloss: "Dear Mr Stoyanov, thank you for Thursday's meeting and for your time. As we discussed, I am attaching the proposal with two implementation options and the results of three similar projects we delivered in Bulgaria over the past two years. If other colleagues are involved in the decision, please let me know whom else to send the documents to. Would a short call on Wednesday 18 March at 10:00 suit you? Kind regards,")*

**Why:** The first name, double exclamation and "no-brainer" become a formal salutation with the surname and "Вие". The "#1" claim turns into local, checkable evidence. The Friday push becomes a dated call and a question about who else decides, which fits slow, layered approvals and keeps the relationship moving.
