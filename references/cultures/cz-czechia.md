# Czechia

## In brief

Czech professional readers expect a calm, factual and formal text: vykání (the formal "you"), "Pan/Paní" or a title, and a correctly declined name in the salutation. Facts are stated plainly, but a "no" or criticism of a person is usually cushioned, and bad news is better delivered by phone than by email. Hype, pathos and pressure to decide quickly put readers off; figures, specifications and a calm, slightly ironic tone build trust. Grammar is a visible test of care: a name left in the nominative ("Vážený pane Novák") or a missing háček is noticed at once. Both "Czechia" and "Czech Republic" are correct; "Czechoslovakia" and "Eastern Europe" are not.

## Voice and tone

- Matter-of-fact and courteous. Courtesy shows through correct address forms and grammar more than through warm adjectives.
- Avoid pathos and grand promises; earnest, heroic wording tends to be read ironically. Understatement works: "to není vůbec špatné" (not bad at all) is real praise.
- Formal letters and emails differ from app and web copy. UI and consumer text should be friendly and plain, with everyday words ("už" not "již", "koupit" not "zakoupit", "nejde" not "nelze").
- Word-for-word translation from English gives stiff, bookish Czech; rewrite from the intent of the paragraph. In English-language mail, use plain international English without idioms or slang.

## Addressing people

- **Vocative is mandatory in salutations:** "Vážený pane Dvořáku", "Vážený pane Svobodo", "Vážená paní Kučerová" (feminine -ová surnames do not change). Foreign names are declined too ("pane Becku"). Templates that insert a nominative name look careless.
- **Function first, then title:** "Vážený pane řediteli", "Vážená paní ředitelko". Without a function, use the highest academic title alone: "pane inženýre", "paní doktorko", "pane profesore". Use title or surname, not both ("paní doktorko Nováková" is wrong).
- Women get feminine titles (ředitelko, inženýrko, doktorko); "paní doktor" sounds outdated. "Paní" covers every adult woman.
- Women's surnames usually end in -ová or -á (Novák / Nováková). Since 2022 a woman may register her surname without -ová, so follow the form the person uses.
- Unknown reader: "Vážené dámy, vážení pánové," or "Vážená paní / vážený pane,"; groups: "Vážení klienti,". In English, "Dear Mr Dvořák" with the háček is safer than first names until invited.
- **"Dobrý den" vs "Vážený/á":** use "Vážený/á" for first contact, official letters, complaints and senior or public-sector readers. "Dobrý den, pane Nováku," is fine for routine email and ongoing threads; "Dobrý den," alone works when the name is unknown.
- The salutation ends with a comma and the next line starts with a lowercase letter. "Vy, Vám, Váš" are capitalised in letters and email.
- **Tykání/vykání:** vykání is the default with new contacts and with support staff as well as managers. Tykání is offered by the senior party (at work, by function; socially, the older person or the woman). Do not switch on your own.
- Academic titles precede the name in signatures and on Czech CVs (Ing. Jana Dvořáková, Mgr. Petr Svoboda, Ph.D.).
- Sign-offs: "S pozdravem" (standard), "S přátelským pozdravem" (warmer), "Se srdečnými pozdravy" (warm), "S přáním pěkného dne" (email). No comma after them; a full signature with role, company and phone follows.

## Structuring a message

1. Specific subject line ("Žádost o cenovou nabídku - kancelářské potřeby"), not "Dotaz".
2. Salutation; introduce yourself if new, or refer to the last call or meeting.
3. The point early, short paragraphs, about one page at most; details in attachments, which are named in the text.
4. A clear ask: what, from whom, by what date.
5. Thanks and an offer to answer questions, then the sign-off.

- One topic per email; a new matter gets a new thread. Confirm verbal agreements in writing; a short recap after each call is appreciated.
- Your contact may need to consult a superior; do not ask for an immediate decision. Expect a reply within one or two working days.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Criticise facts and work, not competence or status. Keep it private; no corrections in group threads.
- Bad news and sensitive issues land better by phone or in person, followed by a written summary.
- Upward criticism is hard for many employees; frame it as a question or proposal and leave room to reply.
- Decode replies: indirect phrases, delay or no answer can mean no. Ask for a concrete next step.
- Praise is sparing; a short, specific thank-you for a result is valued more than effusive compliments. Do not join in when Czechs criticise their own country.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Persuade with figures, technical detail, quality, reliability, references and realistic pricing. An inflated opening offer or artificial urgency hurts credibility.
- Ads may not mislead, even by context, discriminate, offend religious or national feeling or play on fear. The industry code bans belittling competitors or singling out one rival product.
- "Zdarma" (free) only if the customer pays nothing beyond stated delivery; "záruka" (guarantee) only with its terms. Testimonials must be real; living persons need consent. Influencer posts carry an ad label before other hashtags.
- Consumer prices include VAT and fees; consumer information, labels and warranties must be in Czech. Non-essential cookies need prior consent.
- Humour, exaggeration and deadpan irony often work in consumer ads; celebrity faces add little.
- CV: one or two A4 pages, reverse chronological, quantified results, tailored, as PDF. CEFR levels for languages, never overstated. A professional photo is common but optional; leave out marital status, children, health and religion. Drop Czech academic titles on an English CV.
- Job ads must not set age limits ("do 35 let", "mladý kolektiv"), require Czech nationality, unneeded health or fitness, or use a feminine-only title. Write both forms ("právník/právnička"). EU pay-transparency rules are being transposed late; check current status.

## Humor and emphasis

- Dry, ironic and absurd humour is common, and self-irony builds rapport. In early written contact, keep irony out; wordplay rarely survives translation.
- Emphasis comes from precise words and one device (bold), not capitals or "!!".
- Ads must not mock Czech customs such as Ježíšek (the Christmas gift-bringer) or Mikuláš.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Country name:** "Czechia" (Česko) has been the registered short name since 2016; "Czech Republic" (Česká republika) remains the full official name. Both are correct; stay consistent and never write "Czechoslovakia" or merge Czechs and Slovaks.
- Czechia is Central Europe; "Eastern Europe" irritates many readers.
- The communist era, the 1968 invasion and the secret police are painful history; let the Czech side raise them. Official remembrance days (17 November, 21 August, 27 June, 10 June) are poor hooks for promotions.
- A large share of the population declares no religion; do not assume faith in greetings or campaigns.
- State symbols must be used with dignity: no text on the flag, no coat of arms in private branding; fines apply. Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- Chrysanthemums are funeral flowers; bouquets come in odd numbers. Business gifts stay modest.
- Beer and the weekend cottage (chata, chalupa) are everyday, positive images in social and lifestyle contexts.
- Red, white and blue are the national colours; no other colour codes stand out.

## Formats and conventions

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Date | 17. 10. 2026 (spaces after the dots); short 17.10.26. Long: 17. října 2026 (lowercase, month in genitive). Weekdays lowercase. |
| Time | 24-hour, no leading zero: 9:05, 17:30. |
| Numbers | 1 234 567,89 (non-breaking space, decimal comma). Four-digit numbers may stay ungrouped. |
| Currency | 1 234,50 Kč or 1 250 Kč (symbol after amount); CZK in international documents. |
| Percent | 50 % as a noun; 50% sleva or 50procentní as an adjective. |
| Week starts | Monday; ISO week numbers. |
| Phone | +420 601 123 456 (nine digits, groups of three). |
| Address | Dlouhá 12/5 (descriptive/orientation number), 110 00 Praha 1 (postcode 3+2 digits before town). |
| Punctuation | „Czech quotes", en dash with spaces, no em dash. |
| Other | Metric. VAT 21 % standard. 13 public holidays; slow periods are Friday afternoons, July-August and late December. |

## Variation within the culture

- **Generation and sector:** Prague tech, start-ups and international firms move to first names, tykání and English fast. Banks, law, government and traditional industry keep vykání and titles; older managers address each other by title.
- **Language:** English is strong among younger professionals in Prague and other cities; some older readers outside cities know German or Russian better. Outside Prague, Czech-only material is safer.
- **Register by genre:** formal letters use capital Vy and "Vážený"; apps and websites use lowercase "vy" and a conversational tone. Youth brands sometimes use tykání.
- **Slovakia** has been a separate country since 1993; write Czech copy for Czech readers and do not present the two as one market.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Salutation uses the vocative with correct declension; title or surname, not both.
- [ ] First contact and official mail open with "Vážený/á"; "Dobrý den" only for routine email.
- [ ] Comma after the salutation; the body starts lowercase; Vy/Vám/Váš capitalised in letters.
- [ ] Vykání throughout unless the reader has offered tykání; register consistent.
- [ ] Feminine titles for women; surname form as the person uses it; diacritics intact.
- [ ] Specific subject line, one topic, a clear ask with owner and date.
- [ ] Refusals and bad news carry a reason and an offer to talk; no criticism in group threads.
- [ ] No superlatives, hype or artificial deadlines; claims backed by figures.
- [ ] "Zdarma", "záruka" and comparisons meet the advertising rules; prices include VAT.
- [ ] No "Czechoslovakia" or "Eastern Europe"; country name consistent.
- [ ] No flag with text, no misuse of state symbols, no jokes about communist-era history.
- [ ] Job ads: no age, nationality or feminine-only titles; both gender forms used.
- [ ] Dates, numbers, currency and quotes use Czech formats; no em dash.
- [ ] Czech copy is rewritten idiomatically, not translated word for word.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (upbeat US sales style):

> Hi Pavel! Great meeting you Thursday - you're going to love working with us! Attached is our best-in-class offer. We can only hold this amazing price until Friday, so let's get it signed ASAP!

**After** (for a Czech buyer after a first meeting, in Czech with gloss):

> Vážený pane Svobodo,
>
> děkuji za čtvrteční schůzku. Jak jsme se domluvili, posílám v příloze upravenou nabídku. Cena za 500 licencí je 1 180 000 Kč bez DPH, implementace proběhne do 15. ledna 2027 a zahrnuje školení pro 40 uživatelů. Reference ze dvou výrobních firem najdete na straně 4.
>
> Chápu, že nabídku bude posuzovat i vedení. Pokud by to pomohlo, rád připravím krátké shrnutí pro Vašeho ředitele. Mohl bych Vás požádat o předběžnou odpověď do pátku 11. prosince?
>
> S pozdravem

*(Gloss: "Dear Mr Svoboda, thank you for Thursday's meeting. As agreed, I attach the revised offer. The price for 500 licences is CZK 1,180,000 excl. VAT; implementation will be finished by 15 January 2027 and includes training for 40 users. References from two manufacturing firms are on page 4. I understand management will also review the offer. If it helps, I am happy to prepare a short summary for your director. Could I ask for a preliminary answer by Friday 11 December? Regards")*

**Why:** First names, exclamation marks, "best-in-class" and a fake deadline read as pushy. The Czech version uses the vocative ("Svobodo") and capital "Vy", replaces adjectives with price, dates and references, accepts that a superior decides, and asks for a dated answer without pressure.
