# Mexico

## In brief

Mexican business writing is courteous, warm and more formal than US practice: a greeting, a line of goodwill, "usted" and the right professional title (Lic., Ing.) come before any request. A plain "no" feels rude, so refusals arrive wrapped ("déjeme revisarlo") and a polite "sí" is not yet a commitment. Important matters are raised by phone or in person and then confirmed by email. Consumer apps and websites, by contrast, talk to the user with "tú". Readers react badly to stereotypes about cartels, migration or poverty, and law is strict on total prices, verifiable ad claims, job-ad discrimination and privacy notices.

## Voice and tone

- Courteous, diplomatic and warm. Keeping the relationship intact matters as much as the content; curt one-liners and imperatives read as rude.
- Formal letters avoid casual phrases ("nos vemos", "cuídate"). Product and app copy is the opposite: conversational, short, plain verbs ("pedir" rather than "solicitar", "pero" rather than "sin embargo").
- Write Mexican Spanish (es-MX): "computadora", "celular", "costo", "capacitación", "ustedes" (never "vosotros"), and "lo" rather than Spain's "le" for a male direct object ("lo ayudamos"). Neutral Latin American Spanish works for pan-regional content, but emotional ads should be localised for Mexico.
- Some words neutral in Spain are vulgar in Mexico: "coger" means to have sex; use "tomar" or "agarrar". Slang such as "güey" and any double meaning (albur) do not belong in business text; have a Mexican reader check wordplay.
- Diminutives ("un momentito", "un favorcito") soften chat and semi-formal mail but look too casual in formal letters.
- Sales, technical and support material should be in Spanish; English-only material is a disadvantage even when senior staff speak English.

## Addressing people

- Names: given name(s), father's surname, mother's surname (Héctor González López). Use the first surname alone in short form (Sr. González), never the second alone. Many professional women keep their own surnames.
- **Professional titles** are part of correct address in writing: Licenciado/a (Lic.) for university graduates in law, business and many other fields; Ingeniero/a (Ing.); Arquitecto/a (Arq.); Doctor/a (Dr./Dra.) for physicians and PhDs; Contador/a Público (C.P.) and Maestro/a (Mtro./Mtra.) also appear. If the title is unknown, "señor/señora" plus first surname is safe.
- Salutation ends with a colon: "Estimado Lic. González:" / "Estimada Ing. Ramírez:". Name unknown: "A quien corresponda:" or the position ("Estimada Directora General:"). Knowing someone personally does not by itself make an informal greeting right in a formal message.
- "Don/Doña" + first name for older, respected people, more in service and social settings than in corporate email.
- "Usted" with clients, seniors, elders and authorities unless invited to use "tú". Consumer apps, web UI and digital brands address users with "tú" (dropping the pronoun: "Puedes continuar"); banks, insurers, government and luxury brands often keep "usted". Keep one form per text.
- Closings: "Atentamente" (formal), "Saludos cordiales" or "Cordialmente" (standard), preceded by "Quedo a su disposición", "Quedo atento/a a sus comentarios" or the very Mexican "Quedo a sus órdenes". Sign with full name and position.

## Structuring a message

1. Salutation and a goodwill line ("Espero que se encuentre muy bien").
2. Context and relationship, then the request framed as collaboration. Opening straight with the subject reads as a mistake.
3. Where it fits, show how the proposal helps the organisation and the person, not only abstract ROI.
4. Next steps with owners and exact dates, and an offer to talk ("Con gusto le llamo para comentarlo").
5. A closing formula ("Agradezco de antemano su atención"), sign-off and signature.

- For an important first ask, keep demands short and propose a call; then summarise phone and meeting agreements by email.
- A written agenda is read as a proposal; topics may move.
- Name a mutual contact in cold outreach if one exists; introductions through trusted people open doors.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Never criticise anyone publicly, in a reply-all, or in front of their superiors. Deliver bad news privately, with a gentle explanation first.
- Refusals: "Déjeme revisarlo", "Veré qué puedo hacer", "Por el momento no nos sería posible...". When writing for Mexican readers, soften refusals and offer alternatives, but keep the decision findable.
- Decode soft answers: "sí" can mean "I heard you", and "ahorita" or "en un ratito" can mean later or not at all. Follow up with a concrete question ("¿Le funciona el jueves 22 a las 10:00 a.m.?").
- Juniors may not tell a superior that a deadline is unrealistic; ask open questions and make it safe to raise problems.
- Ultimatums, "final offer" language and hard-sell pressure backfire. A first written offer is read as an opening for negotiation.
- Passion in discussion signals commitment, not unprofessionalism. Indirectness varies by region and class; do not overapply it.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Family, tradition, community, celebration, nostalgia and humour are the strongest consumer hooks. Testimonials and local voices persuade more than brand superlatives.
- Consumer law (LFPC, enforced by PROFECO): ads and product information must be truthful, verifiable and clear, including images; an ad that is literally true but exaggerated, partial or artfully framed can still mislead. Comparative ads meet the same standard, and "recommended by" claims need documented backing from the association.
- Consumer prices must show the total payable, including IVA (16%) and all fees; "+ IVA" is normal only in quotes between businesses. Mark MXN when dollars could be meant.
- Packaged food and drinks carry black warning octagons (NOM-051); products with seals may not use cartoons, celebrities or other child-directed imagery, and copy cannot hide or contradict the seals.
- Show pride in Mexican culture authentically; avoid clichés (sombreros, mariachis, cacti, piñatas, tequila) and never use Día de Muertos superficially. Key dates: Día de las Madres (10 May, fixed), Independence (16 Sep), Día de Muertos (1-2 Nov), El Buen Fin (mid-November), Posadas (16-24 Dec).
- Use "estadounidense" rather than "americano" for US people; Mexicans are also Americans in the continental sense.
- CV: usually one page, two for senior roles, opening with a short "perfil profesional". Photo, age and marital status still appear but are optional and contested; multinationals often advise against a photo. CURP and RFC numbers are given at hiring, not on the CV.
- Job ads: the federal labour law (LFT art. 133) bars rejecting candidates for age, sex, marital status, origin, disability, religion, health or similar grounds, and pregnancy certificates are banned. The Supreme Court has treated "sexo femenino, 18 a 35 años, buena presentación" as discriminatory, so drop age ranges, gender and appearance filters unless objectively required. Prefer inclusive nouns ("personas", "personal").

## Humor and emphasis

- Warmth and light humour ease tension in relationship messages; keep teasing and double meanings out of business text.
- Día de Muertos humour about death is part of local tradition; outsiders should not imitate it in brand voice.
- Opening "¡" and "¿" are mandatory; omitting them looks careless. Do not carry English exclamation marks into error or system messages.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Stereotypes**: no jokes or imagery about cartels, violence, poverty, illegal migration, earthquakes or "Montezuma's revenge". Use "migrantes indocumentados", not "ilegales".
- Criticism of crime, corruption or poverty from outsiders offends; do not imply that Mexico is lawless or that bribery is normal.
- The Virgin of Guadalupe has religious and national weight; never treat her irreverently. Religion is otherwise kept out of work messages.
- US-Mexico relations (the 1846 to 1848 war and lost territory, the border, deportations, tariffs) are sensitive; take no side.
- Indigenous peoples: do not use indigenous dress, designs or symbols as costume or decoration. A 2022 federal law protects the cultural heritage of indigenous and Afro-Mexican communities and sanctions unauthorised commercial use; brands have faced public complaints.
- Data protection: a new federal personal data law (LFPDPPP) replaced the 2010 law on 21 Mar 2025, and INAI was dissolved, with its powers passed to the Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno at last check. Forms, cookie banners and job applications need a privacy notice from the moment data is collected; notices that still name INAI are out of date. Check current status of the implementing regulation.
- Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- Marigolds (cempasúchil) belong to Día de Muertos and the dead; keep them out of celebratory imagery. Traditional gift lore links yellow flowers to death and red flowers to spells; white flowers are safe.
- The green, white and red flag and the eagle on a cactus are protected national symbols; reproducing the coat of arms requires government authorisation, so keep them out of logos and merchandise.
- Business imagery: conservative dress for formal contexts; hands on hips can read as aggressive. Avoid black octagon shapes as decoration, since they signal health warnings.

## Formats and conventions

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Date | DD/MM/YYYY (07/03/2026). Long form: 7 de marzo de 2026 (lowercase month); abbreviated ene, feb, mar... |
| Time | 12-hour with a.m./p.m. by default (2:30 p.m.); 24-hour in schedules and transport. |
| Numbers | 1,234,567.89 (period for decimals, unlike Spain's 1.234.567,89). Percent with no space: 25%. |
| Currency | $1,234.50; add "MXN" or "M.N." (moneda nacional) when USD could be meant. |
| Week starts | Sunday. |
| Phone | +52 55 1234 5678. Ten digits nationwide; the old 01, 044 and 045 prefixes are gone and look dated. |
| Address | Street and number, colonia (neighbourhood), postal code, municipality or alcaldía, state. |
| Business hours | Avoid proposing calls at 14:00-16:00 (lunch). "Puente" weekends and mid-December slow replies; the year-end aguinaldo is due by 20 December. |
| Other | Metric. Opening ¿ and ¡ are mandatory. |

## Variation within the culture

- Mexico City, Monterrey and Guadalajara are the most corporate and international markets; Mexico City sets the national standard for copy.
- The north and the nearshoring hubs (Nuevo León, Querétaro, Bajío, Baja California) work closely with US partners and tend to be more punctual; border audiences accept some bilingual copy, but Spanish leads.
- Southern states (Oaxaca, Chiapas, Yucatán) have strong indigenous cultures; some communities have their own greeting norms and may be reserved with outsiders.
- Multinationals and tech firms mix US and Mexican norms; banking, government and family firms keep titles and "usted".
- Younger urban professionals switch to "tú" and first names quickly; start with "usted" and mirror the reply.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (direct US-style):

> Hi Carlos, we can't accept your price. Send a new quote by tomorrow.

**After** (tailored for a Mexican reader, in Spanish with gloss):

> Estimado Lic. Herrera:
>
> Le agradezco mucho la cotización que nos envió. La revisamos con mucho interés; sin embargo, por ahora el monto está un poco por encima del presupuesto que tenemos asignado para este proyecto. ¿Sería posible revisar juntos algunas opciones para ajustarla? Con gusto le llamo mañana para comentarlo.
>
> Quedo a sus órdenes.

*(Gloss: "Dear Mr Herrera (Lic.): Thank you very much for the quote. We reviewed it with great interest; however, for now the amount is somewhat above our budget for this project. Could we review some options together to adjust it? I would be happy to call you tomorrow to discuss. I remain at your service.")*

**Why:** The title, warm tone, softened refusal ("un poco por encima") and offer to talk preserve the relationship while still communicating the price problem. A version for Spain could drop the title, move to "tú" sooner and trim the cushioning.
