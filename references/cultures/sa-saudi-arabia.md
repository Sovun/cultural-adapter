# Saudi Arabia

## In brief

Saudi readers expect respectful, relationship-oriented and religiously aware communication. Islam shapes public life, law and everyday language; honor (sharaf) and family reputation are central. Criticism is delivered privately and indirectly, while praise is generous. The country is changing fast under Vision 2030 (entertainment, women's participation in work, tourism), so assuming ultraconservatism is as wrong as ignoring conservative norms. Public criticism of the royal family, government or Islam is illegal, including on social media and in published content.

## Voice and tone

- Courteous, warm, respectful, and modest. Religious expressions are part of everyday language (Inshallah, Mashallah, Alhamdulillah, "May God give you health") and are used by people of all levels of devotion.
- Arabic content: Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) for formal and official text; Saudi/Gulf dialect for informal social media and consumer campaigns. Check with native writers; machine-translated Arabic is often poor.
- Text must work right to left: layout, icons with direction (arrows, progress bars), number alignment and mixed Arabic/Latin text need RTL review.
- English is widely used in business, especially in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam, and among younger professionals.

## Addressing people

- Names: personal name, father's name, grandfather's name, family or tribal name (Ahmad Hussain Khalid Al-Shammari), often shortened to personal name + family name. "Bin/Ibn" (son of) and "Bint" (daughter of) may appear.
- Address by title + **first name**: "Dr. Ahmad", "Eng. Faisal", "Ustadh Khalid" (teacher/Mr). Royalty and senior officials: "Your Royal Highness", "Your Excellency" (سعادة / معالي depending on rank).
- In Arabic email: "السيد/ أحمد الشمري المحترم" (Mr Ahmad Al-Shammari, respected) or "سعادة الدكتور ..." for senior people; open with "السلام عليكم ورحمة الله وبركاته" and close with "وتفضلوا بقبول فائق الاحترام والتقدير" (formal) or "مع خالص التحية".
- In English: "Dear Dr. Ahmad" or "Dear Mr Al-Shammari"; a first contact can open with "Greetings" or "Assalamu alaikum" if the writer is comfortable doing so.
- Do not ask men about their wives or female relatives, and do not use women's names or images without consent.

## Structuring a message

- Respectful greeting, wishes for wellbeing, acknowledgement of the relationship.
- Background and context, then the proposal; repeat key benefits.
- Provide concrete figures and evidence; avoid hard-sell tactics.
- Close with good wishes and openness to meet; face-to-face meetings are preferred for important matters.
- Schedule around prayer times and Ramadan; the work week is Sunday to Thursday.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Never criticize or correct someone in front of others; doing so attacks their honor. Saudi law also allows prosecution for insulting or defaming someone, including online.
- Combine any critical point with praise; frame as suggestions.
- If offence is caused, acknowledge it; a senior person apologizing can restore the relationship.
- Avoid showing frustration or anger.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Family, hospitality, generosity, honor, national pride (Vision 2030, Saudi achievements) and religious occasions (Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Saudi National Day 23 September, Founding Day 22 February) are strong themes.
- All advertising and media content must respect Islamic values and is regulated by the General Authority for Media Regulation (GAMR/GCAM); media production and publishing may need permits.
- Avoid in imagery and copy: alcohol, pork, gambling, romance or sexual content, immodest dress (cover shoulders, upper arms, knees; tight clothing is inappropriate), same-sex relationships, religious symbols of other faiths, dogs in domestic settings (sensitive for some), and anything mocking religion.
- Never use the name of God (Allah) or Quranic verses decoratively, on disposable items, on clothing or anywhere they could be stepped on or discarded.
- Ramadan campaigns: focus on family, generosity, gatherings, reflection; avoid food/drink imagery in daytime contexts for public spaces.
- Saudization (Nitaqat) shapes hiring; job ads and HR content should reflect Saudi national talent goals.
- CVs commonly include photo, nationality, date of birth and marital status, although international firms follow global formats.

## Humor and emphasis

- Self-deprecating humor and playful teasing among peers are common, but do not joke about family (especially women), religion, the government, sexuality or the country.
- Blasphemy is punishable.
- Emphasis through repetition and eloquent phrasing is normal in Arabic; it may need trimming when adapted for Western readers.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Public criticism of the King, Crown Prince, royal family, government, police, Islam, or Saudi culture is illegal**, including on social media and in content published abroad. Political statements, including on Israel and Palestine, can be treated as national security matters.
- Religion: practicing other religions publicly and proselytizing are illegal. Avoid religious comparison or debate.
- Same-sex relations are illegal; LGBT content cannot be published for Saudi audiences.
- Women's rights: avoid presuming oppression from dress (abaya, hijab, niqab). Acknowledge recent reforms where relevant without patronizing.
- Do not stereotype Saudis as uniformly wealthy, fundamentalist or closed to the world.
- Maps: border and regional disputes (e.g. with Yemen in the past, Gulf naming) can be sensitive; the "Arabian Gulf" is the term used in Saudi Arabia, not "Persian Gulf".

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- The Saudi flag carries the Shahada (Islamic declaration of faith); it must never be flown at half-mast, printed on disposable items, altered, or used on merchandise like T-shirts or footballs.
- Green is associated with Islam and the national flag; use respectfully.
- Left hand is unclean in Islamic custom; show eating, giving and receiving with the right hand.
- Showing the soles of feet is insulting.
- The "OK" hand gesture can be offensive.
- Gold and silk items for men are discouraged in local interpretations of Islam.

## Variation within the culture

- Riyadh and the central Najd region are more conservative; Jeddah and the Hejaz (western coast) are more cosmopolitan; the Eastern Province has a significant Shia minority.
- Younger Saudis (a majority of the population) are digitally native and embrace entertainment, sports and social change; older generations are more traditional.
- About 40% of residents are expatriates (South Asian, Arab, Filipino, Western), with different expectations; content may need multiple languages.

## Review checklist

- [ ] No criticism of the royal family, government, Islam or Saudi culture; no political statements.
- [ ] No alcohol, pork, gambling, romantic or sexual content, immodest dress or LGBT themes in imagery or copy.
- [ ] No decorative use of God's name, Quranic verses or the flag (which carries the Shahada).
- [ ] Criticism is private, indirect and balanced with praise; no public correction.
- [ ] Titles + first names are correct; respectful Arabic opening and closing formulas.
- [ ] Arabic content is professionally written (MSA or appropriate dialect) and RTL layout is checked.
- [ ] Schedules respect prayer times, Ramadan and the Sunday to Thursday work week.
- [ ] "Arabian Gulf" (not "Persian Gulf") in Saudi-facing content.
- [ ] Dates include Gregorian and, where relevant, Hijri equivalents; currency in SAR.
- [ ] Women are depicted modestly and with consent; no stereotyping of Saudi women.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (Western marketing post for a Saudi audience):

> Celebrate Friday night with our new cocktail menu! Grab your date and party till dawn.

**After** (tailored for Saudi readers, in English):

> Weekend gatherings are better together. Discover our new selection of signature mocktails and Arabic coffee blends, perfect for sharing with family and friends.

**Why:** Alcohol, dating and party framing are inappropriate (alcohol is illegal). Family-oriented hospitality, shared gatherings and non-alcoholic signature drinks fit Saudi values and laws.
