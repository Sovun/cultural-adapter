# Romania

## In brief

Romanian professional readers expect a polite, fairly formal first message: "Stimate domnule / Stimată doamnă" with the surname, the plural "dumneavoastră" form, and titles where the reader has them. Trust is personal, so a short courteous opener, a reason for writing and a clear request work better than a cold pitch or pressure. Facts, figures and references persuade; superlatives and self-praise make readers suspicious. Correct Romanian matters: diacritics (ă, â, î, ș, ț with a comma below) are part of the spelling, and ads must carry a Romanian version. History is close to the surface: the communist past, Transylvania and the Hungarian minority, Roma and Moldova all need careful, neutral wording.

## Voice and tone

- Serious, courteous, factual. Casual chumminess in a first message from a stranger reads as a lack of respect; warmth comes once there is a relationship.
- Keep Romanian plain: everyday verbs read better than bureaucratic ones ("faceți" rather than "efectuați", "puteți" rather than "aveți posibilitatea să").
- Translated text often over-uses "dumneavoastră"/"dvs." for every "you/your"; the verb ending already shows the person. "Cu succes" after every confirmation is a calque. Translate the meaning of English idioms instead of hunting for a Romanian equivalent.
- Spelling and diacritics count. A text without ș and ț looks careless in formal and marketing contexts, even though many people drop them in chat.

## Addressing people

- Name order: given name, surname. In the salutation use the surname: "Stimate domnule Popescu," / "Stimată doamnă Popescu,". "Stimate domnule Andrei" (first name) is wrong.
- Use either the name or the function, not both: "Stimate domnule director," or "Stimate domnule Popescu,". Pick the title that fits the matter (director for a business proposal, primar for a city matter).
- Titles carry weight: "doamnă profesor", "domnule doctor", "doamnă director". Keep them with public institutions and in formal settings even after things warm up.
- Write the salutation in full; no "dl" or "dna". Use "doamnă" for all adult women; "domnișoară" sounds dated or patronising.
- Name unknown: address the function ("În atenția Departamentului Resurse Umane") or "Stimate doamne și stimați domni,". "Celor interesați" (to whom it may concern) feels vague.
- "Bună ziua, doamna Popescu," is fine in ongoing business email; keep "Stimată doamnă" for first contact, applications and institutions.
- Pronouns: "dumneavoastră" with plural verbs ("Vă rog să-mi trimiteți...") by default. "Tu" only after mutual agreement and never toward a superior, even a younger one. Answering an unwanted "tu" with "dumneavoastră" is how readers signal distance. Lowercase "dvs." is standard; a capital is optional extra respect.
- Sign-offs: "Cu stimă," (formal standard), "Cu respect,", "Cu mulțumiri," (when you asked for something), "Toate cele bune," (warmer, established contacts).
- In UI and mass mailings avoid "Stimate utilizator" and slash forms like "conectat(ă)"; use "Bună ziua!" and gender-free verb forms ("V-ați conectat").

## Structuring a message

1. Subject line naming the topic and any reference number.
2. Salutation, then one courtesy line ("Vă mulțumesc pentru întâlnirea de marți").
3. Reason for writing ("Vă scriu în legătură cu..."), then facts and figures in short paragraphs.
4. The request or next step with a date, the person who decides, and an offer to call.
5. Thanks and closing formula; list attachments ("Anexe:") in formal letters.

- After a meeting, send a written recap with action items and dates; a positive reply in a meeting may still need approval higher up.
- Signed and stamped documents carry more weight than outsiders expect.
- Headings and buttons use sentence case; months, weekdays, nationalities and languages are lowercase (luni, martie, român).

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- With new partners and seniors, blunt wording reads as bad manners. Give the reason, keep the tone calm and leave a way forward.
- Criticism is private and factual: no corrections in group threads, no adding managers to the CC line on a complaint.
- Suggestions work in the form "Vă sugerăm să..." / "Vă recomandăm să..." (we suggest / recommend that you...).
- Readers may avoid a flat "no". Hesitation or "mai vedem" can be one; ask for a concrete next step and who decides.
- Expect detailed questions on a proposal; scrutiny is caution, not hostility. Do not push for a fast decision, and leave out criticism of Romania or its institutions, even when locals voice it.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- B2B persuasion runs through people: an introduction by a known contact, references, local presence, clear terms. Hard selling and deadlines to "decide today" backfire.
- Replace superlatives with proof: years in market, client names, figures, guarantees. Many consumers enjoy ads as entertainment but distrust their claims, so evidence helps.
- Advertising law bans subliminal ads, content against human dignity or public morals, discrimination by race, sex, language, origin or ethnicity, attacks on religious or political beliefs, and exploiting superstition or fear. Comparative ads must be objective and verifiable. A self-regulatory council offers pre-publication advice.
- Foreign-language ads and public notices need a Romanian translation in letters of the same size; registered brand names are exempt. Product instructions must be in Romanian.
- Commercial email and SMS need prior express consent and a working unsubscribe; the data authority fines firms that cannot prove it.
- CV: at most two pages, reverse chronological, tailored to the post, no superlatives, references on request. Europass suits public institutions and EU programmes; private employers prefer a clean custom layout. A photo is optional; leave out marital status. Write in Romanian for local employers, English for multinationals.
- Job ads: no sex, age, appearance or family-status criteria. The equality body acts on its own initiative and has fined ads asking for "a girl" or setting looks requirements. Use gender-neutral titles or both forms ("ospătar/ospătăriță"). Readers care about salary, stability and benefits. EU pay-transparency rules (salary or range before the interview) were due in June 2026; check Romania's current law.

## Humor and emphasis

- No jokes in a first written contact. Stories and humour come once the relationship exists.
- Exclamation marks suit marketing and packaging, not documentation (except "Atenție!"). No ALL CAPS for emphasis; write "și", not "&".
- Never joke about Roma, ethnic groups or appearance; "funny" job ads have been sanctioned.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Communist past:** the Ceaușescu era splits opinion between nostalgia and memories of repression. No casual references or retro-communist imagery.
- **Identity:** Romanian is a Romance language. Do not group Romania with Slavic countries or the former Yugoslavia, and never conflate Romanians with Roma. Roma face real discrimination; keep stereotypes out entirely.
- **Transylvania and the Hungarian minority:** the region's history (part of Hungary until 1920) and autonomy demands are contested. Use neutral wording and official Romanian place names, adding Hungarian names only for local audiences.
- **Moldova:** the Republic of Moldova is a separate state. Its official language is Romanian (a 2023 law replaced "Moldovan" in its legislation). Do not call Moldovans Romanians or treat the countries as one; "Moldova" also names a historical region of eastern Romania, so make clear which you mean.
- **Extremist symbols:** fascist, legionary (Iron Guard), racist and xenophobic symbols are criminal offences to display or spread; penalties were raised in 2025.
- **Religion:** most Romanians are Orthodox Christians. Ads may not offend religious beliefs; joking use of icons or clergy is risky.
- Corruption is a sore point; avoid hints about "favours" or "how things work here". Disability wording focuses on the person, without "suferind de" (suffering from).
- Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- Flowers come in odd numbers; even numbers are for funerals.
- Key dates: 24 January (Union Day), Orthodox Easter and Pentecost (often weeks after Western Easter), 1 May, 1 June, 15 August, 30 November (St Andrew), 1 December (National Day), Christmas.

## Variation within the culture

- **Hungarian minority:** about 6% of the population, the majority in Harghita and Covasna and large in parts of Transylvania. Minority languages are protected in public life; consumer and HR content for those counties may need a Hungarian version, and some older readers prefer it.
- **Moldova (ro-MD):** shares the language but differs in some vocabulary, many readers also use Russian, and it has its own politics. Do not reuse Romania copy unreviewed or label it "Moldovan".
- **Sectors:** banks, public administration, law and traditional firms keep "Stimate/Stimată", titles and the polite plural; Bucharest and Cluj tech, startups and multinationals use first names, English and sometimes "tu". AI assistants and youth brands increasingly write "tu". State bodies are slower, more formal and more hierarchical than private firms.
- **Generation and migration:** younger professionals and the many with Western work experience expect flatter, faster communication; older readers see early "tu" as impolite.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (fast, US-style follow-up):

> Hi Mihai! Great meeting yesterday. We're the leading provider in the region, so this is a no-brainer. Can you sign off on the deal by Friday?

**After** (to a procurement director after a first meeting, in Romanian with gloss):

> Stimate domnule Dumitrescu,
>
> Vă mulțumesc pentru întâlnirea de ieri și pentru timpul acordat. Așa cum am discutat, vă trimit în anexă oferta revizuită și trei proiecte similare pe care le-am livrat în ultimii doi ani. Dacă este nevoie și de avizul altor colegi, vă rog să-mi spuneți cui să mai trimit documentele. V-ar fi util să stabilim o scurtă discuție telefonică săptămâna viitoare, de exemplu joi, 12 martie, la ora 10:00?
>
> Cu stimă,

*(Gloss: "Dear Mr Dumitrescu, thank you for yesterday's meeting and for your time. As discussed, I am attaching the revised offer and three similar projects we have delivered in the last two years. If other colleagues also need to approve, please tell me whom else to send the documents to. Would a short phone call next week be useful, for example Thursday 12 March at 10:00? Yours sincerely,")*

**Why:** First name, exclamation and "no-brainer" become a formal salutation with the surname and the polite plural. The "leading provider" claim turns into attached evidence. The push to sign by Friday becomes a dated call and a question about who else decides, which respects layered approvals and keeps the relationship moving.
