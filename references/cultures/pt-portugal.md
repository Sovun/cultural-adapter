# Portugal

## In brief

Portuguese professional readers expect a courteous, fairly formal and modest text: titles, set opening and closing formulas, and evidence instead of hype. Warmth grows with the relationship, but first contact is reserved. European Portuguese is not Brazilian Portuguese: vocabulary, spelling, grammar and above all forms of address differ, and copy localised for Brazil reads foreign or careless. Refusals and criticism are softened, and decisions often move up the hierarchy, so patience beats pressure. Consumer information, ads and contracts must be in Portuguese.

## Voice and tone

- Courteous, measured, precise. A Brazilian-style familiar voice ("Oi!", "Valeu!", lots of "!") reads overfamiliar; a stiff legalistic tone reads cold.
- Write European Portuguese (pt-PT). Typical markers to fix in Brazilian copy: telemóvel (not celular), equipa (not time), ecrã (not tela), ficheiro (not arquivo), utilizador (not usuário), palavra-passe (not senha), pequeno-almoço (not café da manhã), contacto, facto, económico.
- Grammar differs too: "estamos a preparar" instead of the gerund "estamos preparando"; pronoun after the verb ("Envio-lhe", not "Lhe envio" or "Te mando").
- Plain modern verbs suit product and marketing copy ("ajudar", not "prestar auxílio"). Old letter formulas ("Venho por este meio...", "Somos a informar que...") fit official letters but sound dated in email.
- Machine translation often mixes the two variants; always request pt-PT and have it reviewed.

## Addressing people

- "Você" is the main trap. In Portugal it can sound distant or even rude, especially to older or senior readers. In formal text drop the pronoun and use the third-person verb ("Pode confirmar...?"), or use "o senhor / a senhora" or the title ("O Eng. Costa poderá...").
- "Tu" is for friends, peers and young, casual consumer brands. Banks, utilities and public bodies use the pronoun-free third person.
- Titles: "Dr./Dra." for any university graduate, not only doctors; also "Eng./Eng.ª", "Arq./Arq.ª", "Prof./Prof.ª". Use the title the person signs with, in the correct gender form; if you do not know it, "Sr./Sra." is safe.
- Names: usually two or more surnames, mother's before father's. The last surname is the one used alone (Ana Sousa Ferreira is "Dra. Ferreira"), the reverse of Spanish logic.
- Openers: "Ex.mo Senhor / Ex.ma Senhora" (official letters, most formal first contact); "Caro/Cara + title + name" (standard business); "Estimado/a" (warm formal); "Caro/a colega" (colleagues); "Olá, Rita" or "Bom dia, Rita" once you know each other. Unknown recipient: "Ex.mos Senhores". "Prezado/a" exists but reads Brazilian as an everyday default.
- Closings: "Com os melhores cumprimentos" (standard), "Cumprimentos" (ongoing contact), "Atentamente" (formal). "Um abraço" and "Beijinhos" are only for close contacts, never new business partners.
- Avoid "Vossa Excelência" outside official correspondence; it looks over-ceremonious in a job application or sales email.

## Structuring a message

1. A subject line that names the topic (for applications: job title and reference).
2. Titled salutation and, for first contact, one line saying who you are and why you write ("Na sequência da nossa conversa...").
3. The request or proposal, then reasons and figures in short paragraphs; attachments mentioned in the text.
4. Next steps with dates, and an offer to clarify ("Fico ao dispor para qualquer esclarecimento").
5. A thank-you line ("Desde já agradeço") and the closing, with a full signature (name, role, company, phone with +351).

- Keep one register from salutation to sign-off.
- After calls and meetings, send a written summary of what was agreed. Expect answers to take time while the proposal goes up the chain; a polite reminder is better than a deadline ultimatum.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Soften: "Infelizmente, neste momento não nos é possível..." (unfortunately, right now we are unable to...), give a reason, and propose an alternative.
- Criticism goes privately and is about the work, not the person. Do not widen the CC list on a complaint.
- Read soft signals: hesitation, vague answers or silence may hide disagreement. Ask an open question or suggest a call.
- Juniors rarely contradict seniors in writing; no objection is not consent.
- When adapting German, Dutch or US text, add a cushion and a reason; when adapting Brazilian text, keep the softness but remove the effusive warmth.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Persuade with track record, references, quality, guarantees and clear terms. Stability, reliability and long-term value beat "disruption" language.
- Ad law: factual claims must be provable on request; comparative ads must be objective and must not denigrate. Ads may not misuse national or religious symbols, discriminate, use someone's image without consent, or carry political or religious messages. Strict rules apply to minors, alcohol, gambling, unhealthy food and medicines.
- Language: ads, labels, manuals, packaging and other consumer information must be in Portuguese (foreign text fully translated); consumer contracts, invoices and receipts too. Retail prices are shown as the final price with VAT included.
- Promotional email or SMS to individuals needs prior express consent, given to your company specifically; existing customers may get offers for similar products with an easy opt-out in every message.
- CV: about two pages (more for senior, academic or technical roles), reverse chronological, action verbs with concrete results, text-based PDF. Photo optional; city and country are enough as address. A short tailored cover letter or application email.
- Job ads must not state or imply a sex preference, age limit, nationality or appearance requirement ("boa apresentação") unless genuinely essential for the job, and must not ask about health, pregnancy or family plans. Use neutral or paired titles ("Engenheiro/a", "pessoa responsável"). Pay is not yet required in ads; EU pay-transparency rules are still being transposed, so check their status.

## Humor and emphasis

- Irony and wordplay are common socially but come only after rapport in business writing. Never joke about the reader or their country.
- Diminutives ("um minutinho", "cafezinho") sound affectionate and informal; leave them out of formal text.
- Emphasise with bold or a clear sentence, not capitals or "!!". Saudade and nostalgia are real emotional registers; use them with care, not as a gimmick.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Portugal is not Spain:** no Spanish words, "Hispanic" or "Latino" labels, or Iberian lumping. Spanish is not an acceptable substitute language.
- **Brazil:** do not present Brazilian Portuguese as "the" Portuguese or treat the two markets as one.
- **Estado Novo and colonial history:** the dictatorship (until 1974), the colonial wars and the colonial past are serious subjects; no light marketing use or "discoveries" glorification without care.
- **Religion:** Catholic identity and Fátima matter to many; irreverent religious imagery in ads is risky and religious content in ads is restricted.
- Defamation is a criminal offence and is aggravated when public; be careful with negative statements about named companies or people.
- Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- The red carnation stands for the 25 April 1974 revolution and still carries political meaning; avoid it as decoration.
- Chrysanthemums are associated with funerals and graves.
- The flag, anthem and coat of arms must not be used disparagingly, nor at all in alcohol ads.
- Imagery should show a realistic mix of people; Lisbon trams, fado and custard tarts are clichés if overused.

## Formats and conventions

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Date | DD/MM/YYYY (17/10/2026); short 17/10/26. Long: 17 de outubro de 2026 (months and weekdays lowercase). |
| Time | 24-hour: 14:30; "14h30" is common in prose. |
| Numbers | 1 234 567,89 (space as thousands separator, comma decimal). Four-digit numbers not grouped (2500). |
| Large numbers | Long scale: "mil milhões" = 10^9; "bilião" = 10^12 (Brazilian "bilhão" = 10^9). Prefer "mil milhões". |
| Currency | 1 234,50 € (symbol after amount). Never "R$" or dot grouping. |
| Percent | 25% |
| Week starts | Monday in business calendars (standard locale data lists Sunday; check software defaults). |
| Phone | +351 21 234 5678 (Lisbon/Porto landlines); +351 912 345 678 (mobiles start with 9). Nine digits. |
| Address | Rua Augusta, 100, 2.º Esq. / 1100-053 Lisboa. Postcode NNNN-NNN before the town. |
| Other | List separator ";". Lowercase after a colon. 1990 spelling reform (ação, direção). Metric. VAT 23 % mainland, lower in Madeira and the Azores. |

## Variation within the culture

- **Sector:** Lisbon tech, start-ups and multinationals use first names, "tu" internally and often English. Banking, law, insurance, government and family firms keep titles and the third person.
- **Generation:** younger readers accept "você" and informal tone more readily; older readers expect "o senhor / a senhora" and titles, and react most to Brazilianisms.
- **Region:** perception of "você" differs between regions; the north and interior are often more traditional than Lisbon. The Azores are one hour behind the mainland; Madeira is on mainland time.
- **Language:** English is widely understood by younger professionals, but Portuguese materials are appreciated and legally required for consumers.
- **Calendar:** August is the main holiday month and the Christmas to New Year period is slow. Lunch is a long, relationship-building meal.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Text is European Portuguese: pt-PT vocabulary, "estar a + infinitive", pronoun after the verb, 1990 spelling.
- [ ] No "você" as the default address; pronoun-free third person, title or "o senhor / a senhora" in formal text, "tu" only for casual consumer brands.
- [ ] Titles (Dr., Eng.) used where the reader holds them, with correct gender form; surname is the last one.
- [ ] Salutation and closing match one register ("Caro/a ... / Com os melhores cumprimentos").
- [ ] Purpose appears after at most one introductory line; next step has a date.
- [ ] Refusals and criticism are cushioned, give a reason and stay private.
- [ ] No pressure for an immediate decision; time allowed for consultation upward.
- [ ] No hype or unprovable superlatives; comparisons objective and non-denigrating.
- [ ] Brazilian-level exclamations, emoji, diminutives and "Abraços/Beijinhos" removed from business text.
- [ ] Consumer copy, labels, manuals and contracts fully in Portuguese; prices include VAT.
- [ ] Marketing email or SMS lists rest on prior, company-specific consent with an opt-out.
- [ ] Job ad has no sex, age, nationality or appearance requirement and no health or family questions.
- [ ] Formats: 1 234,50 €, DD/MM/YYYY, "mil milhões", +351 phones, NNNN-NNN postcodes.
- [ ] Nothing treats Portugal as Spain or Brazil; no flippant use of the dictatorship, colonial past or religion.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (Brazilian Portuguese, informal, reused for Portugal):

> Oi, Ricardo! Tudo bem? Você poderia me mandar o arquivo com os dados técnicos até sexta? Estamos finalizando a proposta e o time precisa disso urgente! Valeu, abraços!

**After** (for a Portuguese engineer met once, in pt-PT with gloss):

> Caro Eng. Ricardo Lopes,
>
> Na sequência da reunião de terça-feira, estamos a concluir a proposta técnica. Para cumprirmos o prazo acordado, seria possível enviar-nos o ficheiro com os dados técnicos até sexta-feira, dia 23? Se for mais prático, posso ligar-lhe amanhã para esclarecer algum ponto.
>
> Desde já agradeço a colaboração.
>
> Com os melhores cumprimentos,

*(Gloss: "Dear Eng. Ricardo Lopes, Following Tuesday's meeting, we are finalising the technical proposal. To meet the agreed deadline, would it be possible to send us the technical data file by Friday the 23rd? If easier, I can call you tomorrow to clarify any point. Thank you in advance for your help. Best regards,")*

**Why:** The request and deadline stay clear, but "você", "Oi", "Valeu" and "abraços" are gone, the title and surname are used, and Brazilian words (arquivo, time, finalizando) become pt-PT (ficheiro, equipa or a rewrite, "estamos a concluir"). Urgency is replaced by a reason and a precise date, and the offer to call keeps the relationship warm.
