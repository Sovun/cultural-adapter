# Greece

## In brief

Greek business readers expect a courteous first message in the polite plural, with "κύριε/κυρία" and the surname, and a relationship that then moves fast to a warmer tone. Email supports personal contact rather than replacing it: important requests go with an offer to call or meet, and the real decision usually sits with the owner or the most senior person. Ideas can be argued openly, but criticism of a person stays private and pressure tactics backfire. Timelines flex, August and Orthodox Easter stop business, and dates in writing should be confirmed. Neighbour disputes (Türkiye, Cyprus, North Macedonia) and the debt crisis are sensitive, and Greek text has its own punctuation, including ";" as the question mark.

## Voice and tone

- Courteous, warm, clear. A cold, scripted message reads as distant; gushing reads as insincere.
- Modern Greek prefers plain everyday words, active verbs and short sentences. Learned, old-fashioned vocabulary and long relative clauses make text sound bureaucratic; machine translation often produces exactly that.
- In instructions, drop the English "please": the polite plural imperative is already courteous ("Πληκτρολογήστε τον κωδικό"). In letters and emails, "παρακαλώ" in a request is normal.
- Exclamation marks are rare in professional and UI text and never close an error message. Use a full stop.
- Write first business contacts and consumer material in Greek where possible. English is the main foreign business language and younger managers use it easily; older decision-makers may prefer Greek documents.

## Addressing people

- Name order: given name, surname. Many full names include the father's name in the genitive between them (Γεώργιος Δημητρίου Παπαδόπουλος); it is not a surname and is not used in address. Since 1983 married women keep their birth surname, so do not assume a couple shares one.
- Formal address: "κύριε" / "κυρία" + surname, with the vocative form for men (Κύριε Παπαδόπουλε). Female surnames and surnames ending in -ου do not change (Κυρία Παπαδοπούλου, Κύριε Νικολάου). Abbreviations: κ. and κα.
- Salutations: "Αξιότιμε κύριε / Αξιότιμη κυρία [surname]," for first contact or seniors; "Αγαπητέ / Αγαπητή [first name or surname]," once you know each other. A bare "Γεια σας" or first name is too casual for a first business email. In English: "Dear Mr Papadopoulos".
- "Κυρία Μαρία" (title + first name) is a common middle register between colleagues and with clients you know.
- Academic titles are not needed in ordinary business address; use Dr or Professor in academia, medicine and where the person signs with it. Introductions pair the name with the role, so put the job title in signatures.
- Sign-offs: "Με εκτίμηση," (standard formal), then full name and role. "Φιλικά," or "Χαιρετισμούς," between established contacts.
- Name days (the feast of the saint one is named after) are often celebrated as much as birthdays. A short "Χρόνια πολλά!" (many happy returns) on a contact's name day is well received; it also serves for birthdays, Christmas and Easter.

## Structuring a message

1. Subject ("Θέμα") naming the topic and any date.
2. Salutation and one courtesy line ("Σας ευχαριστώ για τη χθεσινή συνάντηση").
3. Purpose early ("Σας γράφω σχετικά με..."), then the essentials in short paragraphs or bullets; detail in attachments ("Όπως θα δείτε στο συνημμένο...").
4. A concrete request with a date, softened ("όταν το επιτρέπει το πρόγραμμά σας", when your schedule allows), plus an offer to call or meet.
5. Thanks ("Θα εκτιμούσα ιδιαίτερα την απάντησή σας") and closing.

- A cold email should aim to arrange a call or meeting, not to close a deal. After a call, send a short written recap of who does what by when; confirm meetings the day before. Contracts and specifications should be explicit and complete; a Greek translation of key documents helps.
- Formal letters use sender block and date, "ΠΡΟΣ:" (to) and "ΥΠΟΨΗ:" (attention of) lines, then salutation and body.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Lively, even heated debate about ideas is normal and usually leaves no grudge. Silence or a sudden terse reply from a usually talkative contact is the worrying signal.
- Criticism of a person's competence or integrity is private: no corrections in group threads or with extra people in copy. Keep it on the issue, never sarcastic.
- Refusals to outsiders get a reason and an alternative. Read vague answers ("θα το δούμε", we'll see) as a probable no and ask for a concrete next step.
- Ultimatums, hard closing deadlines and early "best and final" offers backfire; expect gradual movement from both sides. Leave criticism of Greece, its economy or its politics to Greek speakers.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Persuade with trust: a shared contact, known clients, years in the market, local support, guarantees and clear specifications. Show how the proposal helps the reader's own standing and results.
- Consumer ads can be warm and use family, home and community. Humour works best when it carries real information.
- Advertising follows a national self-regulatory code backed by law: claims must be true and provable on request, comparisons fair and verifiable, competitors and groups never ridiculed. "Δωρεάν" (free) only when nothing is hidden; free trials that turn paid must say so upfront. Testimonials must be genuine; paid endorsements and influencer posts must be clearly marked as advertising. No exploiting fear or superstition, extra care with children, no discrimination.
- Marketing email and SMS need prior opt-in; the exception is existing customers buying similar products, with a free, easy opt-out. The data protection authority fines senders to bought lists.
- CV: one or two pages, reverse chronological, tailored to the ad, no "I" sentences, achievements in numbers, PDF in the language of the ad. A photo is optional; many male candidates state military service status. Cover letter: one page, about four paragraphs, enthusiasm welcome, self-deprecation not.
- Job ads may not mention sex or family status, set age limits ("20-35 ετών") or use other discriminatory criteria. From November 2026 job titles and ads must be gender-neutral, candidates are entitled to the starting pay or range before the interview, and asking for pay history is banned; check current details. Avoid one-gender job titles ("σερβιτόρα") without the other form.

## Humor and emphasis

- Avoid jokes in a first written contact and in formal or product text; humour comes with familiarity. Emphasise with bold, not capitals or "!!". Headings and buttons use sentence case, not English Title Case.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Türkiye:** island, sea and airspace disputes in the Aegean are sensitive; never show contested boundaries on maps and do not present Greek food, music or symbols as Turkish (or the reverse). In Greek, Istanbul is normally "Κωνσταντινούπολη" and İzmir "Σμύρνη"; this is standard usage, not a political statement, so do not "correct" it. In English and in content for mixed audiences, use the current official names.
- **Cyprus:** an independent EU state, divided since 1974. Take no position; do not list or map the north as a separate country in Greek-facing material.
- **North Macedonia:** the 2018 agreement gave the neighbouring state this name. Use the full name; "Macedonia" alone in Greece means the Greek region around Thessaloniki. Some readers still use older names; do not adopt them.
- **Debt crisis and austerity:** keep out of business copy, with no jokes or stereotypes about finances or work ethic. Migration is politically charged.
- **Orthodox Church:** most Greeks identify as Orthodox and see it as part of national identity even if they rarely attend. Blasphemy stopped being a criminal offence in 2019, but mocking religion still causes strong backlash and the ad code bans religious discrimination. Artefacts held abroad (the Parthenon sculptures) are a live issue.
- Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- The open palm pushed toward someone ("μούντζα") is a grave insult: avoid raised-palm "stop" or high-five images facing the viewer, and the 🖐 emoji aimed at a person. The OK circle sign can also read as obscene.
- A backward head tilt means "no"; confirm agreement in words, not nods, on video calls.
- Blue evil-eye charms ("μάτι") are familiar protective symbols. Knives as gifts are bad luck; keep business gifts modest.
- Orthodox Easter usually falls on a different date from Western Easter: plan campaigns on the Orthodox calendar. 25 March and 28 October are national holidays.

## Formats and conventions

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Date | Short d/M/yy (7/3/26); 07/03/2026 in forms. Long: 7 Μαρτίου 2026 (month in the genitive after a day; "Μάρτιος" alone). Days and months are capitalised. |
| Time | Business text uses 24-hour: 14:30. The standard locale default is 12-hour (2:30 μ.μ., with π.μ./μ.μ.), seen in software and speech. |
| Numbers | 1.234.567,89; four-digit numbers are grouped (1.234). |
| Currency | 1.234,50 € (symbol after, with a space). "ευρώ" is invariable. |
| Percent | 25% (no space). |
| Week starts | Monday; ISO week numbers. |
| Phone | +30 21 0123 4567 (Athens landlines start 21); other landlines 2xxx xxxxxx; mobiles +30 69x xxx xxxx. Ten digits, no trunk prefix. |
| Address | Σταδίου 10 / 105 64 Αθήνα: street, then number; five-digit postcode (Τ.Κ.) written 3+2, before the town. |
| Punctuation | ";" is the question mark; « » for quotes; em dashes rare (use commas or brackets). Sentence-case headings. |
| Other | Metric. VAT (ΦΠΑ) 24 % standard rate; consumer prices include it. Office hours roughly 9:00-17:00; public offices open earlier. |

## Variation within the culture

- **Athens and Thessaloniki vs the regions:** corporate Athens, multinationals, shipping and tech move faster, write more in English and use first names sooner. Provincial firms and family SMEs keep more formality, more phone contact and slower decisions led by the owner or eldest relative.
- **Generations:** younger professionals mix registers in email and use the singular "εσύ" sooner; brands aimed at young consumers often address them in the singular, while B2B, banking and public-sector text stays plural. Older readers expect "κύριε/κυρία" and Greek documents.
- **Calendar:** August, Holy Week through the week after Easter, and roughly 20 December to 6 January are slow. Avoid calls to private numbers during the afternoon rest (about 15:00-18:00).
- **Cyprus (el-CY):** Greek-language content also reaches Greek Cypriots, but Cyprus is a separate state with its own law, institutions and idioms. Neutral standard Greek is the safe default; do not reuse Greek legal or regulatory copy unchanged.
- **Minorities:** Greece officially recognises a Muslim minority in Thrace, many of whose members identify as Turkish; the label is politically sensitive, so use the term the audience uses. Albanian and other migrant communities are large.

## Review checklist

- [ ] First contact uses the polite plural, "Αξιότιμε κύριε / Αξιότιμη κυρία" with the correct vocative, and "Με εκτίμηση".
- [ ] Register (εσείς or εσύ) is consistent throughout; the junior side does not switch to "εσύ" first.
- [ ] Greek ";" replaces "?"; « » for quotes; no English Title Case in headings.
- [ ] Long dates use the genitive month (7 Μαρτίου 2026); business times are 24-hour; prices read "1.234,50 €" with VAT included.
- [ ] Purpose appears after one courtesy line; the request has a date and an offer to call or meet.
- [ ] The message goes to the real decision-maker, not only to a junior contact.
- [ ] Criticism is private and about the issue; no sarcasm, no extra people in copy.
- [ ] No ultimatums, hard closing deadlines or pressure for an instant answer.
- [ ] Plain modern Greek: no stiff learned vocabulary, no "please" or "!" in instructions and error messages.
- [ ] Claims are provable; comparisons fair; "δωρεάν" only without hidden conditions; ads and influencer posts are labelled.
- [ ] Marketing email or SMS goes only to opted-in contacts and has an easy opt-out.
- [ ] Job ads have no age, sex or family-status criteria, use gender-neutral titles, and plan for a pay range.
- [ ] Nothing takes sides on Türkiye, Cyprus or North Macedonia; maps show no contested boundaries; "North Macedonia" in full.
- [ ] No debt-crisis jokes, religious mockery, open-palm or OK-sign imagery.
- [ ] Deadlines and launches avoid August, Orthodox Easter week and national holidays.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (terse Northern European style):

> We need your comments on the draft agreement by Wednesday. If we don't hear back, we will assume you agree.

**After** (to a new distribution partner after a first meeting, in Greek with gloss):

> Θέμα: Σχέδιο συμφωνίας διανομής - σχόλια έως Πέμπτη 29 Οκτωβρίου
>
> Αξιότιμε κύριε Νικολάου,
>
> Σας ευχαριστώ για τη συνάντηση της Τρίτης στη Θεσσαλονίκη. Όπως συμφωνήσαμε, σας στέλνω συνημμένο το σχέδιο της συμφωνίας διανομής.
>
> Θα εκτιμούσα ιδιαίτερα τα σχόλιά σας έως την Πέμπτη 29 Οκτωβρίου, ώστε να ολοκληρώσουμε την τελική έκδοση πριν από τη συνεδρίαση του διοικητικού μας συμβουλίου στις 3 Νοεμβρίου. Αν προτιμάτε να συζητήσουμε κάποια σημεία τηλεφωνικά, είμαι στη διάθεσή σας όποτε σας εξυπηρετεί.
>
> Με εκτίμηση,

*(Gloss: "Subject: Draft distribution agreement - comments by Thursday 29 October. Dear Mr Nikolaou, thank you for Tuesday's meeting in Thessaloniki. As agreed, I am sending you the draft distribution agreement attached. I would greatly appreciate your comments by Thursday 29 October, so that we can complete the final version before our board meeting on 3 November. If you would rather discuss some points by phone, I am at your disposal whenever suits you. With regards,")*

**Why:** "Silence means consent" reads as a pressure tactic and would likely stall the deal. The deadline stays, but now with a reason, a full date and an offer to talk. The Wednesday deadline moved because 28 October is a national holiday. The polite plural, "Αξιότιμε κύριε" and "Με εκτίμηση" fit a first exchange; once Mr Nikolaou writes "Αγαπητέ" and a first name, the next email can relax to match.
