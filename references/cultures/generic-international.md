# Generic international (unknown or mixed audience)

## In brief

Use this profile when the reader's culture is unknown, mixed, or not yet covered by a dedicated profile. It is not a culture: it is a set of **low-risk defaults** that are unlikely to offend anyone and easy to read for non-native speakers. It aims for the middle on every dimension, with a bias toward courtesy, clarity and restraint. When a dedicated profile exists, it always wins over this one.

## Dimension profile

| Dimension | Score | What it means for a text |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Directness | 3 | State requests and problems clearly, but cushion negatives with appreciation and an alternative. Never blunt, never so soft that the point disappears. |
| D2 Context reliance | 3 | Be explicit about actions, owners and dates; skip patronizing over-explanation. |
| D3 Formality | 3 | Polite and neutral. Use title + surname in first contact if the name order is clear; otherwise use the full name as written by the reader. Mirror their style afterwards. |
| D4 Hierarchy | 3 | Show respect to senior readers without excessive deference; do not bypass someone's manager. |
| D5 Relationship first | 3 | One courteous opening line, then business; a warm closing line. |
| D6 Expressiveness | 2 | Calm and positive. Avoid strings of exclamation marks, superlatives and emotional language. |
| D7 Self-promotion | 2 | Let facts, numbers and references carry the claim; avoid "best", "world-class", "revolutionary". |
| D8 Group orientation | 3 | Credit the team as well as individuals; frame benefits for both the reader and their organization. |
| D9 Time precision | 4 | Give exact dates with month names and time zones. |
| D10 Argument style | 2 | Short context sentence, then the key point, then supporting details. |

## Voice and tone

- Clear international English (or the reader's language if known): short sentences, common words, active voice.
- No idioms, sports metaphors, slang, sarcasm or culture-specific jokes.
- Courteous markers ("please", "thank you", "I would appreciate") in moderation.

## Addressing people

- Use the reader's name exactly as they write it, including diacritics and order.
- If unsure of gender, avoid Mr/Ms: "Dear Alex Kim" or "Hello Alex Kim".
- If unsure of name order (East Asian names, single names), use the full name.
- Keep titles (Dr., Prof., Eng.) the reader uses in their signature.
- Neutral sign-offs: "Kind regards", "Best regards".

## Structuring a message

- Greeting, one courteous line, purpose, details, clear next step with date, polite close.
- Use headings or bullets for anything longer than three short paragraphs.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Acknowledge effort or the request first, state the issue factually, offer an alternative.
- Criticize work, not people. Never criticize anyone publicly in a group email.
- Avoid absolute words ("wrong", "unacceptable"); prefer "does not meet X because Y".

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Benefits backed by evidence; avoid absolute claims, which are illegal in some markets.
- Imagery: diverse, modest, no religious or national symbols, no alcohol unless clearly appropriate.
- CVs: avoid unnecessary personal data (age, marital status, religion, photo) unless the target market expects it.

## Humor and emphasis

- Leave humor out unless the relationship is established.
- Emphasis through structure (bold key facts) rather than exclamation marks or capitals.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- Avoid politics, religion, ethnicity, territorial disputes, wars and historical conflicts unless they are the subject.
- Maps and country lists: avoid unless needed; disputed territories are sensitive in many markets.
- Do not use national flags as decoration.
- Run [universal checks](../framework/universal-checks.md) section 5 (legal) for every target market the text will reach.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- Avoid hand gestures in imagery (OK sign, thumbs up and V-sign are offensive somewhere).
- Avoid number-based gimmicks (4, 13, 17 are unlucky in different places).
- Avoid white or black flowers and funeral-associated colors in celebratory content.

## Formats and conventions

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Date | 7 March 2026, or ISO 2026-03-07. Never ambiguous 03/07/2026. |
| Time | 24-hour with time zone and UTC offset: 14:30 CET (UTC+1). |
| Numbers | Avoid ambiguity; for large numbers write "1.2 million". |
| Currency | ISO code: EUR 1,250 or USD 1,250. |
| Units | Metric, with imperial in brackets for US readers. |
| Week | Do not assume Monday to Friday; check the reader's work week for deadlines. |

## Variation within the culture

Not applicable: this profile is deliberately culture-neutral. If the reader's culture becomes known, switch to its profile or the nearest one.

## Review checklist

- [ ] No idioms, slang, sarcasm, sports or war metaphors.
- [ ] Dates use month names or ISO format; time zones are stated.
- [ ] Salutation does not assume gender or name order.
- [ ] Negatives are cushioned but still clear; an alternative is offered.
- [ ] No superlatives or absolute claims without evidence.
- [ ] No political, religious, historical or territorial references.
- [ ] No gestures, flags or symbolic numbers in imagery.
- [ ] Sentences are short and readable for non-native speakers.

## Rewrite example

**Before:**

> Hey guys!! Our killer new release is a total home run, you'll love it. Ping me ASAP if you wanna hop on a call 3/7.

**After:**

> Hello everyone, our new release is now available. Early users report that it cuts report preparation time by about 30%. If you would like a short demo, please reply by 7 March and I will arrange a 20-minute call at a time that suits your time zone.

**Why:** Slang, sports metaphors, the violent "killer", stacked exclamation marks and an ambiguous date are all risky for an unknown audience. The rewrite stays friendly while being clear and neutral.
