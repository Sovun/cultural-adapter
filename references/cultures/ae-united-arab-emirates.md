# United Arab Emirates

## In brief

The UAE combines conservative Emirati Arab-Islamic values with one of the world's most international populations: roughly nine in ten residents are expatriates, and English is the everyday business language. Content therefore has two audiences: Emirati citizens (Arabic, family- and heritage-oriented, respectful of rulers and religion) and a multinational expat majority. Respect, hospitality and relationships matter, and UAE law strictly regulates public decency, online speech and media activity. Ambition and "world's first/biggest/best" framing are part of the national brand, especially in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

## Voice and tone

- Respectful, polished, aspirational. Government communication emphasizes vision, innovation, tolerance, happiness and national achievement.
- Arabic (Modern Standard Arabic for official text; Emirati/Gulf dialect for social campaigns) and English are both used; bilingual content is standard for public-facing material. Arabic requires right-to-left layout.
- Many readers are non-native English speakers (South Asian, Arab, Filipino, European): use clear international English, avoid idioms and culture-specific slang.

## Addressing people

- Emirati names: given name + bin/bint (son/daughter of) + father's name + family or tribal name (Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum). Short form is given name + family name.
- Address by title + first name in speech ("Dr. Ahmed", "Sheikh Saeed"); in formal letters to officials use "Your Excellency" (سعادة / معالي by rank) or "Your Highness" (سمو) for members of ruling families.
- Arabic email: open with "السلام عليكم" or "تحية طيبة وبعد"; close with "مع خالص التحية والتقدير".
- English email: "Dear Mr Ahmed", "Dear Dr. Fatima"; first names are common among expats.
- Do not ask Emirati men about wives or female relatives; do not use women's photos without consent.

## Structuring a message

- Greeting and courtesy, then purpose; keep it concise for busy senior readers.
- Align proposals with national strategies (UAE Vision, Net Zero, AI strategy, diversification) where relevant: this signals relevance and respect.
- Follow up with calls and meetings; WhatsApp is widely used.
- Schedule around prayer times, Ramadan and the work week (Monday to Friday, with Friday afternoon off for many government entities).

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Never criticize Emirati counterparts in public; be indirect and respectful.
- Express disagreement as alternative options; preserve face for senior people.
- Avoid public complaints about employers, companies or individuals online: UAE cybercrime and defamation laws apply to posts and reviews.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Luxury, innovation, safety, family, hospitality, ambition and records ("first", "largest", "tallest") resonate. Claims must be accurate; consumer protection law penalizes misleading advertising.
- **Media licensing**: producing and distributing media content, and paid influencer activity, require permits/licences from the UAE Media Council. Content must not offend Islam, the state, rulers, national unity or public morals.
- Avoid imagery with immodest dress, public displays of affection, alcohol (permitted in licensed venues in most emirates, but banned in Sharjah), pork, gambling, and LGBT themes for public campaigns.
- Respect national symbols (flag, UAE National Day 2 December, Commemoration Day 30 November, Eid holidays).
- Ramadan campaigns: generosity, family gatherings (iftar), reflection; tone down entertainment and food imagery during fasting hours.
- Emiratisation quotas affect hiring; HR content should reflect them.
- CVs often include photo, nationality and visa status; international norms dominate in multinationals.

## Humor and emphasis

- Light, friendly humor is fine with expats; avoid humor involving religion, rulers, the state, families or women.
- Swearing and rude gestures, including online and in messages, are illegal.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- Criticism or insult of the rulers, the federal or emirate governments, national symbols or Islam is illegal and enforced, including on social media and private messaging.
- Political commentary on regional conflicts (Israel-Palestine, Iran, Yemen, Qatar relations) is risky.
- Same-sex relations are illegal; cross-dressing is illegal. Public displays of affection can lead to arrest.
- Unlicensed fundraising and charity appeals are illegal, including online.
- Photographing people without consent, especially women, can lead to arrest.
- The body of water is called the **Arabian Gulf** in the UAE. Three islands (Abu Musa, Greater and Lesser Tunb) are disputed with Iran; UAE-facing maps should not show them as Iranian.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- National colors: red, green, white, black. The flag must be shown correctly and treated respectfully.
- Right hand for giving, receiving and eating; left hand is considered unclean.
- Falcons, Arabic coffee (gahwa), dates, desert and heritage architecture are positive cultural symbols.
- Avoid images of dogs in Emirati homes and religious symbols of other faiths in mainstream public campaigns.

## Variation within the culture

- **Dubai**: cosmopolitan, commercial, fast-moving and most liberal in lifestyle.
- **Abu Dhabi**: capital, government-centric, more formal; large state-linked investment entities.
- **Sharjah**: most conservative (alcohol ban, stricter decency rules); strong cultural and Islamic identity.
- Northern Emirates (Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah) are smaller and more traditional.
- Expat communities (Indian, Pakistani, Filipino, Egyptian, British and others) may need content in their languages and reflect their own norms.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (Western nightlife promo for a UAE-wide audience):

> Ladies' night! Free drinks, bikini pool party, kiss at midnight for good luck.

**After** (tailored for a UAE audience):

> Weekend sunset sessions at the pool: live music, signature mocktails and a family brunch on Saturday. Book your table now.

**Why:** Public displays of affection are illegal, immodest framing and free-alcohol promotions are inappropriate for broad UAE audiences (and alcohol is banned in Sharjah). Family-friendly leisure framing fits both Emirati and expat audiences.
