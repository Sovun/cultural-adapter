# Türkiye

## In brief

Turkish business culture is warm, hospitable, relationship-based and hierarchical. Initial communication is polite and indirect; directness increases as trust grows. Respect is shown through the "first name + Bey/Hanım" form of address. National pride is strong and protected by law: insulting the Turkish nation, flag, the President or Atatürk is a criminal offence, including online. Turks are not Arabs; confusing the two is a common and resented error.

## Voice and tone

- Warm, respectful, courteous. Phrases of goodwill are frequent ("Kolay gelsin" (may it be easy), "İyi çalışmalar" (have a good work day)).
- Use Turkish for consumer communication; English is common among younger, educated and international business audiences.
- Turkish-specific characters (ç, ğ, ı, İ, ö, ş, ü) must be preserved. Note the dotted and dotless i: uppercase of "i" is "İ", lowercase of "I" is "ı". Software that applies English case rules corrupts Turkish text (the "Turkish I problem").
- Official English name of the country is "Türkiye" (UN registration since 2022). Use "Türkiye" in formal and government-related content; "Turkey" is still widely understood. Never confuse with Arabic, Persian or Greek identity.

## Addressing people

- Names: given name + family name (Mehmet Yılmaz). Surnames only since 1934; many are descriptive.
- Respectful address: **first name + Bey** for men (Mehmet Bey), **first name + Hanım** for women (Ayşe Hanım). This is the standard business form, even with long-standing contacts.
- Formal written salutation: "Sayın Mehmet Yılmaz," or "Sayın Yılmaz,". Common email: "Merhaba Mehmet Bey," / "Ayşe Hanım merhaba,".
- Professional titles are used: Doktor, Profesör, Avukat (lawyer), Mühendis (engineer), Müdür Bey (Mr Manager).
- Sign-offs: "Saygılarımla," (with my respects, formal), "İyi çalışmalar," (friendly professional), "Teşekkürler,".
- In English: "Dear Mr Yılmaz" or "Dear Mehmet Bey".

## Structuring a message

- Courteous greeting and some social warm-up.
- Clear, well-presented proposal with visuals; emphasize mutual benefit and also prestige and influence the partnership brings.
- Expect follow-up by phone; email responses can be slow.
- Leave room for bargaining; never lead with your best offer.
- Avoid expiring offers and high-pressure deadlines.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Criticize indirectly and privately, ideally through a third party for serious matters. Criticism of a person extends to their family and country.
- Refusals are polite and often vague; a non-committal answer may mean no.
- Give a genuine reason if you decline an invitation or offer; a flat refusal can offend.
- Turks bargain hard; feigned disinterest is a known tactic.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Family, hospitality, national pride, tradition and modern aspiration resonate.
- The Advertising Board (Reklam Kurulu) enforces truthful advertising; comparative claims must be substantiated. KVKK (Personal Data Protection Law) governs consent.
- Alcohol advertising is heavily restricted. Respect Ramadan and religious holidays (Ramazan Bayramı, Kurban Bayramı) in campaign timing and tone.
- Avoid meeting or launch scheduling in July/August and during Ramadan.
- CVs often include a photo and date of birth; international firms follow global norms.
- References and track record matter, since individuals may undersell themselves.

## Humor and emphasis

- Relaxed humor and playful teasing are common among acquaintances.
- Do not joke about the nation, flag, anthem, Atatürk, the President or religion.
- Exclamation marks are acceptable in friendly text.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Article 301** (public denigration of the Turkish nation, state institutions, military, police) and **Article 299** (insulting the President) are actively enforced, including for social media posts made abroad. Insulting Atatürk is also a crime (Law 5816). Defacing banknotes and insulting the flag are illegal.
- **Armenian genocide**, **Kurdish question/PKK**, **Cyprus**, **relations with Greece** (including Aegean islands), and **Nagorno-Karabakh** are highly sensitive; Turkish official positions differ from those of many other countries. Avoid in commercial content.
- Politics (President Erdoğan, the 2016 coup attempt, opposition, journalists) is polarized and risky.
- Secularism vs religiosity is a social divide; do not assume all Turks are conservative Muslims, nor that they are not.
- LGBT events have been banned since 2015; LGBT themes in mainstream marketing face regulatory and public backlash.
- Do not overemphasize danger or instability in content about Türkiye.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- The Turkish flag (red with white crescent and star) must be shown correctly and respectfully; misuse is a crime.
- The **OK hand sign** carries an offensive homosexual connotation; the "fig" gesture is obscene.
- The evil eye bead (nazar boncuğu) is a widespread protective symbol, positive in imagery.
- Showing soles of feet and pointing are rude.
- Tulips and Ottoman motifs are positive national design references.

## Variation within the culture

- Istanbul, Izmir and Ankara are modern and cosmopolitan; Central, Eastern and Southeastern Anatolia are more traditional and religious.
- Kurdish-majority regions in the southeast have distinct language and culture; Kurdish-language content can be seen as politically sensitive.
- Large Turkish diaspora in Germany, the Netherlands and Austria; their norms blend both cultures.
- Many businesses are family-owned; multinationals and tech firms are more Western in style.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (direct ultimatum):

> This is our final offer and it expires Friday. Take it or leave it.

**After** (tailored for a Turkish reader, in English):

> Dear Mehmet Bey,
>
> Thank you again for the warm welcome in Istanbul last week. Based on our discussions, we have prepared the attached proposal, which we believe creates strong value for both companies and positions you well in the new regional market. We would be glad to discuss any points with you, and I will call you on Tuesday if that is convenient.
>
> With my respects,

**Why:** Ultimatums and deadline pressure backfire in Turkish negotiations and can end the relationship. Warmth, the respectful "Bey" address, mutual benefit and an open invitation to discuss keep the door open for the expected bargaining.
