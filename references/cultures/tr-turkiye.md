# Türkiye

## In brief

Turkish business readers expect a warm, courteous first message, the polite "siz" in Turkish, and "first name + Bey/Hanım" or "Sayın" as address. Directness grows with trust; early on, criticism and refusals are cushioned and a vague answer may mean no. Decisions sit at the top, often with a family owner, and ultimatums or expiring offers backfire. Insulting the nation, the state, the flag, the President, Atatürk or religious values is a criminal matter, also online. Marketing faces strict rules on claims, comparative ads, consented messaging and influencer labels.

## Voice and tone

- Warm, respectful, courteous. Goodwill formulas are common, "İyi çalışmalar" (have a good working day) above all.
- In Turkish, "siz" (formal plural) is the default for customers, B2B, banks and government; "sen" fits youth brands, taglines and calls to action. Never mix both in one text.
- Rewrite from meaning, not word for word. Stiff Ottoman-Arabic connectors (vasıtasıyla, ivedilikle) read old-fashioned in brand copy; everyday words (aracılığıyla, hızla) read friendlier.
- Do not over-apologise. "Üzgünüz" sounds odd in service copy; use "maalesef" or "ne yazık ki" for minor issues and "özür dileriz" for serious ones.
- Consumer copy must be in Turkish; English works with educated, younger and international business readers.
- Use "Türkiye" in English texts for Turkish readers: the state asks for it in all correspondence (circular of December 2021), and the UN adopted it in 2022. "Turkey" is still understood elsewhere. Turks are not Arabs and Turkish is not Arabic; mixing them up is resented.
- Proverbs and phrases such as "inşallah" are ritual formulas; recognise them when decoding, do not imitate them in business English.

## Addressing people

- Names: given name + family name (Mehmet Yılmaz).
- Standard respectful form: **first name + Bey** (Mehmet Bey) or **first name + Hanım** (Ayşe Hanım), even with long-standing contacts. It attaches to the first name: "Yılmaz Bey" is a classic foreign error.
- Formal or first-contact salutation: "Sayın Mehmet Yılmaz," or "Sayın Yılmaz,"; use "Sayın" + full name if gender is unclear and "Değerli Yetkili," if no name is known. Semi-formal: "Merhaba Mehmet Bey," or "Ayşe Hanım merhaba,".
- Titles are used and may replace the name: Doktor, Profesör, Avukat (lawyer), Mühendis (engineer), Hocam (for academics and respected seniors), and rank forms like "Müdür Bey" (Mr Manager).
- Rank shows in set phrases: "Bilgilerinize arz ederim" (submitted for your information) goes upward; "rica ederim" (I request) to peers or juniors.
- Sign-offs must fit the message: "Saygılarımla," (with my respects, formal), "Saygılarımızla," when writing as a company, "İyi çalışmalar," or "Teşekkürler," for routine mail only.
- In English: "Dear Mr Yılmaz" is safe; "Mr Mehmet" mirrors Turkish usage and is also fine.
- Avoid "bayan" as a noun for women ("bayan eleman"); "kadın" is the neutral word.

## Structuring a message

- First contact: greeting, a short relationship line or the name of who referred you, then the purpose. Repeat contacts can be more direct.
- Subject line summarises the message (e.g. "Toplantı Talebi: 15 Şubat 14.00"); state what each recipient should do; name attachments in the body.
- Proposals: concise, visual, mutual benefit and long-term partnership; mention the prestige the partnership brings. Turkish and English versions are appreciated.
- Expect follow-up by phone or WhatsApp; email replies can be slow. A polite reminder after a couple of days is fine; pushing harder is not.
- Leave room for bargaining; never lead with your best offer. Concessions land best framed as a gesture to the partner.
- No expiring offers or high-pressure deadlines. Whole sentences in capitals read as shouting.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Criticise privately, starting with appreciation. Never correct a senior where others can see it, including cc lists and group chats. Business criticism is taken personally.
- Refusals are polite and often vague. A bare "Bunu yapamam" (I can't do this) is harsh; "Şu an bunu üstlenmem mümkün değil" (not possible for me right now), a short reason or an alternative sounds professional.
- Concerns may come back later or through others rather than in the meeting.
- Bargaining is expected and opening prices can move a lot.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Family, hospitality, national pride, local symbols and tradition resonate; price and instalment options (taksit) weigh heavily, especially with high inflation.
- Advertising Regulation (under Consumer Protection Law 6502, enforced by the Advertising Board): verifiable claims need scientific evidence; "best" or "number 1" claims without proof are fined. Comparative ads may not name or show competitors, their logos or trade names.
- Prices in ads include taxes and are in TL; discount ads show the previous price, dates and any stock limit. Small print may clarify but not contradict the main claim. Vague green claims and pressure on children are not allowed.
- Influencers must label paid or gifted content "Reklam" or "Tanıtım", visible without "read more" and on every share; the advertiser shares liability. Fake or AI-generated likenesses of real people implying endorsement are banned (check current status).
- SMS, email and call marketing to consumers needs prior opt-in recorded in İYS; each message identifies the sender (with MERSİS number), offers a free opt-out, and carries a "reklam" or "kampanya" tag where not obvious. Pre-ticked boxes are invalid.
- KVKK (Law 6698): at last check, the privacy notice and the explicit consent text must be separate; one "I have read and accept" box is treated as unlawful.
- Alcohol advertising is largely banned (Law 4250). Respect Ramadan and the Bayrams in timing and tone.
- Job ads: Labour Law art. 5 and Law 6701 bar filters on sex, age, marital status, religion, ethnicity and similar grounds unless the job truly requires it; job sites have been fined too.
- CVs commonly include a photo, birth year, marital status and, for men, military service status; leave out birthplace and family details. Multinationals often accept CVs without a photo. References are usually "available on request".
- Professionals may undersell themselves; ask for references and track record.

## Humor and emphasis

- Relaxed humor and teasing are common once a relationship exists.
- Remove, do not soften, jokes about the nation, state, flag, anthem, Atatürk, the President or religion: all carry criminal exposure.
- Exclamation marks are used less than in English; emphasise with one bold word, not capitals.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Turkish Penal Code:** art. 299 (insulting the President) and art. 301 (denigrating the nation, state institutions, military, police) are actively enforced, including against social media posts; art. 300 protects the flag and anthem; art. 216 punishes publicly degrading religious values; art. 217/A targets false information spread to cause panic, so avoid alarmist copy on health, earthquakes or the economy.
- **Law 5816** protects Atatürk's memory, images and monuments; use his image respectfully, never in parody. 10 November (his death anniversary) calls for a sober tone and no sales push.
- **The events of 1915**, the Kurdish question and PKK, Cyprus and Aegean disputes with Greece are highly sensitive; Turkish official positions differ from many other countries'. Take no position in commercial content and show no contested boundaries on maps.
- Politics is polarised; stay out of it. Religion and the headscarf are not topics for early contact.
- Observance ranges from secular to devout; do not assume either. Do not infer Kurdish identity from a name or region.
- LGBT themes face regulatory and public backlash; Pride marches have been banned in İstanbul since 2015 (check current status).

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- The flag (red, white crescent and star) must be shown correctly: no logos over it, no cropping, no use as a discount background.
- The OK hand sign can read as offensive and the "fig" gesture is obscene; avoid both in imagery. Showing the soles of the feet is rude.
- Tea and Turkish coffee signal hospitality; the evil eye bead (nazar boncuğu) and tulips are familiar positive motifs.
- Do not present Turkish food, music or symbols as Greek or Arab, or the reverse. In Turkish, the standard names are İstanbul and İzmir; Greek-language names for these cities are normal in Greek and not a statement.

## Variation within the culture

- İstanbul, İzmir and Ankara are more cosmopolitan and English-friendly; central and eastern Anatolia are more traditional and religious.
- Kurdish is widely spoken in the east and southeast.
- Family-owned firms concentrate decisions in the founder or eldest family member; multinationals and tech firms are flatter and more direct.
- Younger private-sector writers move between "Merhaba Ad Bey" and "Sayın"; institutions keep rank formulas like "arz ederim".
- Large Turkish diaspora communities in Germany, the Netherlands and Austria blend both cultures.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (direct ultimatum):

> This is our final offer and it expires Friday. Take it or leave it.

**After** (tailored for a Turkish reader, in English):

> Dear Mr Yılmaz,
>
> Thank you again for the warm welcome in İstanbul last week. Based on our discussions, we have prepared the attached proposal, which we believe creates strong value for both companies and positions you well in the new regional market. We would be glad to discuss any points with you, and I will call you on Tuesday if that is convenient.
>
> With my respects,

**Why:** Ultimatums and deadline pressure backfire in Turkish negotiations and can end the relationship. A respectful address, thanks for the hospitality, mutual benefit and an open invitation to discuss keep room for the expected bargaining.
