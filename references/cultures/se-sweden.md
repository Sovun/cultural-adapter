# Sweden

## In brief

Swedish readers expect plain, calm, factual text and the informal "du" with first names for everyone, including senior people and strangers. Hype, superlatives and pressure undermine trust; modest claims backed by facts persuade. Decisions are built by consensus (förankring: anchoring a decision with everyone affected), so texts should invite input, be easy to forward and leave time for internal discussion rather than push for a quick yes. Compared with Finland, the tone is just as low-key but more consensus-seeking: disagreement is framed as an open question, and "we" matters more than "I". Equality, non-discrimination and honest marketing are backed by law and strong social expectation.

## Voice and tone

- Plain, calm, concrete. Short sentences and everyday verbs: "få" rather than "erhålla", "kunna" rather than "ha möjlighet att". Stiff officialese is treated as poor writing; public bodies are legally bound to plain language (klarspråk) and companies follow the same ideal.
- Hyperbole is read literally. "The best solution on the market" invites the question "says who?"; "used by 40 Swedish municipalities" works.
- Informal address does not mean closeness. Keep topics professional; friendliness shows through courtesy and reliability.
- Use gender-neutral wording in generic references: plurals, roles ("kunden", "den anställda"), or "hen" (in the official dictionary since 2015, though a few readers still find it marked). For a real person, use their own pronoun.
- Most professionals read English well, but consumer and public-facing content should be in Swedish.

## Addressing people

- Name order: given name, surname. Use the first name from the first message; "herr", "fru" and academic titles are not used in address. The royal family is the one remaining exception.
- "Du" is the default in business, marketing, UI and customer service since the du-reform of the late 1960s. Singular "ni" is not a safe polite form: many readers hear distance, condescension or a hint that they are old. Use "ni" only as plural you, for a group or a company ("Tack för att ni hörde av er").
- Openings: "Hej Anna," (standard, from first contact), "Hej!" or "Hej alla," (informal or group), "Bästa Anna," (a little more formal). "Dear Sir/Madam" or "Bäste herr Lind" read stiff and dated; in English, "Hi Anna," is the right level.
- Sign-offs: "Med vänlig hälsning" or "Vänliga hälsningar" (standard), "Hälsningar" (lighter), "MVH" only in quick internal mail.

## Structuring a message

1. Specific subject line.
2. Greeting and at most one courtesy line ("Tack för senast", thanks for the last meeting, or "Tack för ditt mejl").
3. Purpose in the first paragraph, then background, figures and honest risks or limitations. Headings and bullets help.
4. A clear next step with a date, plus room for input: who else should see this, and when a decision is realistic.
5. Short, forward-looking close ("Hör gärna av dig om du har frågor").

- Meeting requests go out well ahead with an agenda; topics not on it are rarely decided.
- Proposals should be self-contained and easy to forward, since several people will weigh in. Say who decides and by when, so the process does not read as open-ended.
- Do not expect replies in the evening, at weekends or during holidays. Avoid deadlines and launches in July, the sports holiday weeks (February to March) and around Christmas.

## Disagreeing, refusing, giving feedback

- Disagree on the issue, calmly and with facts. Raised tone, sarcasm or blame damages the exchange more than in most of Europe.
- Frame objections as a shared question: "Jag undrar om vi har räknat med..." (I wonder whether we have accounted for...), "Kan vi titta på ett alternativ?".
- A refusal gives the reason and, where possible, an alternative. A blunt "no" without explanation reads as unfriendly, even though Swedes value clarity.
- Read understatement upward: "inte helt fel" (not entirely wrong) means right; "lite dyrt" (a bit expensive) may mean far too expensive. A short, delayed or neutral reply can signal more disagreement than it shows.
- A consensus "yes" can mean "we can live with it", not enthusiasm. Once a decision is reached it is implemented and rarely reopened, so do not reargue it later.
- Ultimatums, artificial deadlines and repeated chasing backfire. When adapting Swedish text for hierarchical cultures, make the decision and the decision-maker explicit; when adapting Finnish or Dutch text for Sweden, soften verdicts into questions.

## Persuasion, marketing and self-presentation

- Persuade with facts, test results, references, sustainability proof and honest limitations. Modest wording and an understated close beat urgency.
- All marketing must be recognisable as marketing and show who is behind it, influencer posts included. Misleading claims and leaving out material facts are banned; comparative ads must compare like with like on verifiable features, without denigration. The Consumer Ombudsman can seek large fines.
- Generic green claims ("miljövänlig", "klimatneutral" through offsetting) are banned under EU rules applying from 27 September 2026 unless backed by recognised proof; own sustainability labels need certification. Readers are wary of greenwashing anyway.
- Consumer marketing email needs prior consent (existing customers and similar products excepted); every marketing email, B2B too, needs an opt-out.
- Sexist or stereotyped portrayals are the most common complaint to the advertising self-regulator. Show equality naturally: men in caring roles, women leading.
- CV: one to two pages, tailored, clear headings, short summary at the top. A photo is optional. Leave out date of birth, personal identity number (personnummer), marital status and nationality. Show qualities through concrete situations, not adjective strings ("flexibel, driven, noggrann").
- Cover letter (personligt brev): one page at most, written for this job, personal but not private, opening with which job and why you fit. Parental leave explains a gap without apology.
- Job ads must not signal preference by sex, gender identity, ethnicity, religion, disability, sexual orientation or age: no "ung", no gendered titles, no "native Swedish". State which Swedish skills (spoken, written) the tasks need. The EU pay-transparency rules had not been transposed as of late 2026 and the government paused the bill; check the current status before promising or omitting salary ranges.

## Humor and emphasis

- Humour is dry, understated and self-ironic, often unmarked. It can help a relationship email but rarely belongs in corporate or product text.
- Emphasis comes from restraint and specifics. No all caps; at most one exclamation mark, usually in the greeting.
- Avoid jokes about ethnicity or national stereotypes, and do not lump the Nordic countries together.

## Sensitive topics and legal red lines

- **Discrimination:** seven protected grounds apply to all recruitment and employment. Once a complaint is plausible, the employer must show it was not discriminatory, so written rejections should give neutral, job-related reasons. Employers also do yearly pay mapping between women and men.
- **Personal data:** GDPR applies; ask for personnummer only when truly needed and use account or customer numbers in examples.
- **State symbols:** the state coat of arms and the flag may not be used in trademarks or to suggest official endorsement without permission.
- **Languages:** Swedish is the main language by law; Finnish, Meänkieli, Sami, Romani Chib and Yiddish are national minority languages with rights in public services.
- **Immigration and integration** divide opinion; do not assume the reader's view. Lagom and Jante clichés in ads aimed at Swedes often read as foreign.
- Legal items here are pointers, not legal advice.

## Symbols, numbers, colors and imagery

- Nature, outdoor life, fika (the coffee break with colleagues) and design are authentic, positive themes; luxury and status display clash with expectations of moderation.
- Use diverse, non-stereotyped people in imagery and examples.

## Variation within the culture

- **Generations:** older readers who lived through the du-reform are the most put off by singular "ni"; some younger service workers use it as politeness. In writing, "du" stays the safe default.
- **Public vs private sector:** authorities must write plain Swedish and serve minority-language speakers; companies have more freedom and often work in English internally, especially in tech and multinationals in Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö.
- **North:** Sami, Finnish and Meänkieli are used in parts of Norrbotten; public-facing content there may need minority-language versions. Do not use Sami dress or symbols as decoration.
- **Region:** Skåne, Gothenburg, Norrland and Stockholm have distinct identities; avoid treating Sweden as uniform or Stockholm as the whole country.
- **Neighbours:** Swedish expectations are not Finnish or Danish ones. Finnish readers accept blunter verdicts and a firmer clock; Swedish readers expect more consultation and softer framing of disagreement.

## Rewrite example

**Before** (written with a confident, top-down sales default):

> Dear Mr Bergström, our platform is the market-leading solution and will cut your costs dramatically! We need your decision by Friday to guarantee this exclusive price.

**After** (for a Swedish IT manager, in Swedish with gloss):

> Hej Johan,
>
> Tack för samtalet i tisdags. Här är förslaget vi pratade om. I tre liknande kommuner minskade supportärendena med i snitt 18 % första året; siffrorna och två kontaktpersoner finns i bilagan. Priset gäller till 30 november, så ni har tid att gå igenom det med teamet. Vill du att vi tar ett kort möte med fler av er nästa vecka?
>
> Vänliga hälsningar,

*(Gloss: "Hi Johan, thanks for the call on Tuesday. Here is the proposal we discussed. In three similar municipalities, support tickets fell by 18 % on average in the first year; the figures and two contacts are attached. The price is valid until 30 November, so you (plural) have time to go through it with the team. Would you like a short meeting with more of you next week?")*

**Why:** First name and "du" replace the title. The superlative becomes a checkable figure with references, and the Friday ultimatum becomes a realistic validity date that leaves room for the team to agree. Offering to meet more people supports the consensus process instead of pressing one person for a yes.
