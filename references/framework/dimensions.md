# Communication Dimensions

Every culture profile scores the culture on the same ten dimensions. The scores are ratings on a 1 to 5 scale. They are not survey data and should not be quoted as such.

A score describes the **default expectation of a typical professional reader** in that culture. It is a starting point for review, not a rule about individuals. Age, sector, region, company culture and personal history can move any reader a long way from the default.

The scale was chosen so that each dimension maps to something a reviewer can actually change in a text.

## D1. Directness

How plainly disagreement, refusals, criticism and bad news are stated.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Negatives are implied. "That may be difficult" can mean no. Criticism is wrapped, delayed or delivered through a third party. |
| 2 | Negatives are softened with several cushions and usually framed as questions or suggestions. |
| 3 | Mixed. Clear statements are fine but are expected to be balanced with positives. |
| 4 | Negatives are stated clearly with light cushioning. |
| 5 | Blunt is polite. Padding reads as evasive or wasteful. |

**Text levers:** hedging words, softeners ("perhaps", "it might be worth"), feedback sandwich, passive voice for blame, how a "no" is phrased.

## D2. Context reliance

How much of the meaning is expected to live outside the words: in the relationship, the setting, the history and what is left unsaid.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Low context. Say everything explicitly. Repetition and summaries are welcome. |
| 3 | Moderate. Key points explicit, some background assumed. |
| 5 | High context. Over-explaining feels condescending; readers infer intent from tone and omission. |

**Text levers:** level of detail, explicit action items, recaps, spelling out the obvious, reading between the lines of the source text.

## D3. Formality

The default register of written communication with someone you do not know well.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | First names immediately, casual openers, contractions, emoji acceptable in business. |
| 3 | Polite neutral register; first names after brief contact. |
| 5 | Titles, surnames, formal pronouns and set phrases are expected; casualness reads as disrespect. |

**Text levers:** salutation, pronoun choice (T/V distinction), contractions, slang, emoji, sign-off.

## D4. Hierarchy sensitivity

How much rank, age and status shape who may say what to whom.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Flat. Juniors challenge seniors openly; titles are rarely used. |
| 3 | Rank matters for decisions but not much for tone. |
| 5 | Tone, wording and even the channel depend on the relative status of writer and reader. |

**Text levers:** who is copied, how a request to a senior is framed, use of titles, deference markers, whether to bypass a hierarchy.

## D5. Relationship before task

Whether trust is built through personal connection before business, or through competence and delivery.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Task first. Get to the point in the first line; personal remarks are optional. |
| 3 | A short courtesy opener, then business. |
| 5 | Personal warmth, enquiries about wellbeing and relationship history come first; a cold, purely transactional message damages trust. |

**Text levers:** opening lines, closing warmth, personal references, length of the "small talk" part of a message.

## D6. Emotional expressiveness

How much visible emotion, enthusiasm and intensity is normal in professional text.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Restrained. Superlatives and exclamation marks look unprofessional or insincere. |
| 3 | Moderate warmth; occasional enthusiasm is fine. |
| 5 | Expressive. Flat text reads as cold or unhappy. |

**Text levers:** exclamation marks, intensifiers ("amazing", "thrilled"), emoji, emotional vocabulary.

## D7. Self-promotion tolerance

How readers react to claims of excellence about yourself, your team or your product.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Modesty expected. Strong self-praise erodes credibility; let facts and third parties speak. |
| 3 | Confident claims are fine if backed by evidence. |
| 5 | Bold claims are normal. Understatement is read as low ambition. |

**Text levers:** superlatives ("best", "world-class"), "I" statements in CVs, testimonials versus self-claims, comparative advertising.

## D8. Group orientation

Whether credit, responsibility and identity are framed around the individual or the group.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Individual. "I achieved", personal choice, standing out. |
| 3 | Mixed. |
| 5 | Collective. "We", team, family, community, harmony and belonging. |

**Text levers:** pronouns, attribution of credit, marketing appeals (personal freedom versus family/community benefit).

## D9. Time precision

How literally deadlines, schedules and commitments are read.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Flexible. Dates are intentions; relationships can move them. |
| 3 | Deadlines matter but some slack is normal. |
| 5 | A date is a promise. Vague timing ("soon", "early next week") is a problem. |

**Text levers:** precision of dates and times, confirmation of agreements, how delays are announced.

## D10. Argument style

How a case is built and in what order the reader expects it.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Conclusion first. Lead with the recommendation, then supporting points ("bottom line up front"). |
| 3 | Either order works if clearly signposted. |
| 5 | Context first. Background, reasoning and principles before the conclusion; jumping straight to the ask feels abrupt or unfounded. |

**Text levers:** paragraph order, executive summary placement, subject lines, how quickly the ask appears.

## How to use the scores in a review

1. Compare the **source culture** (or the writer's apparent style) with the **target culture** on each dimension.
2. A gap of **2 or more points** on a dimension is a strong signal that the text needs adjustment on that lever.
3. A gap of **1 point** is worth a light touch only if the text type is sensitive (see [text-types.md](text-types.md)).
4. Always prefer the profile's concrete guidance and checklist over the raw score.
