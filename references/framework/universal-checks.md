# Universal Checks

These checks apply to any text adapted for a culture other than the one it was written in, before the culture-specific checklist. They catch the most common cross-cultural failures, which are often not about tone at all but about hidden assumptions.

## 1. Language and register

- [ ] The target language variant is correct (en-US vs en-GB, pt-BR vs pt-PT, es-MX vs es-ES, zh-Hans vs zh-Hant, Bahasa Indonesia vs Malay).
- [ ] Formal and informal address (T/V distinction: tu/vous, du/Sie, ty/Ви, tú/usted) is chosen deliberately and used consistently.
- [ ] Honorifics and titles follow the target culture's conventions (see each profile's "Addressing people").
- [ ] The text does not mix registers (a formal letter that ends with a casual sign-off, or vice versa).

## 2. Idioms, metaphors and references

- [ ] Idioms and sports metaphors are removed or localized ("touch base", "ballpark", "hit it out of the park", "sticky wicket", "move the goalposts").
- [ ] Pop-culture, holiday and historical references are meaningful to the target reader (Thanksgiving, Super Bowl, Black Friday, Bonfire Night).
- [ ] Violent or military metaphors ("killer feature", "attack the market", "blow up", "bomb") are checked for war-affected audiences.
- [ ] Wordplay and puns either work in the target language or are replaced.
- [ ] Humor, irony and sarcasm are only kept where the target culture and text type tolerate them.

## 3. Names, people and identity

- [ ] Name fields and salutations do not assume "first name + last name" (single names, family-name-first order, patronymics, multiple surnames).
- [ ] Gender is not assumed from names; gender-neutral salutations are available.
- [ ] Identity terms (ethnicity, religion, disability, sexuality) use current, respectful terms in the target culture.
- [ ] Imagery and examples reflect the target audience rather than defaulting to the source culture's demographics.

## 4. Formats

- [ ] Dates are unambiguous (month spelled out in international text; target format otherwise).
- [ ] Time uses the target clock convention and names the time zone for cross-border scheduling.
- [ ] Numbers use the target decimal and grouping separators (and lakh/crore or 万/億/억 grouping where relevant).
- [ ] Currency symbol, placement and code are correct; ambiguous "$" is clarified.
- [ ] Units are converted (imperial vs metric), including sizes, temperatures and distances.
- [ ] Addresses, phone numbers and postal codes follow local structure.
- [ ] Week start, work week and weekends are correct (Sunday to Thursday in Israel and Saudi Arabia; Monday to Friday in the UAE since 2022).
- [ ] Right-to-left layout is handled for Arabic and Hebrew.

## 5. Legal and regulatory flags

- [ ] Advertising claims (superlatives, "No. 1", environmental and health claims) meet target-market rules.
- [ ] Mandatory local language requirements are met (e.g. France, Poland, Ukraine, Indonesia, Quebec).
- [ ] Data collection and consent wording fits local privacy law (GDPR, PIPL, LGPD, KVKK, NDPA).
- [ ] Employment texts avoid discriminatory criteria under local law, and request only allowed personal data.
- [ ] Maps, borders and disputed place names follow the target market's legal and political expectations.
- [ ] Nothing in the text could violate speech laws in the target country (insulting the state or leaders, blasphemy, totalitarian symbols).

## 6. Symbols, colors, numbers and gestures

- [ ] Colors used for emphasis or branding do not carry unwanted meanings (white for mourning in East Asia, purple in Brazil, green hats in China).
- [ ] Numbers in prices, product names and sets avoid local bad-luck numbers (4 in East Asia, 13 in the West, 17 in Italy).
- [ ] Hand gestures in imagery are safe (OK sign, thumbs up, V-sign, fig sign).
- [ ] Religious and national symbols are not used decoratively.

## 7. Calendar and timing

- [ ] Launch and send dates avoid major religious holidays, national days of mourning and the target region's holiday periods (Ramadan, Golden Week, Chinese New Year, August in France, Shabbat for religious Israeli audiences).
- [ ] Seasonal references match the hemisphere ("summer sale" in December for Australia and Brazil).

## 8. Readability for non-native readers

- [ ] Sentences are short and unambiguous when the target reader is likely reading in a second language.
- [ ] Phrasal verbs and contractions are reduced in international English.
- [ ] Acronyms are expanded on first use.
- [ ] One term is used consistently for one concept.

## Severity guide

| Severity | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Legal exposure, safety risk, or deeply offensive content. Must fix before publishing. | Insulting a head of state where that is a crime; Nazi or communist symbols; "Polish death camps"; showing Taiwan as a country in mainland China content. |
| Major | Likely to damage trust or the relationship, or to cause misunderstanding. | Blunt refusal to a Japanese partner; hype in a German B2B offer; wrong honorific; ambiguous dates. |
| Minor | Sounds foreign or slightly off, but the message gets through. | US spelling for UK readers; one exclamation mark too many; slightly too formal salutation. |
| Suggestion | Opportunity to resonate better. | Adding a seasonal greeting; using a local proof point. |
